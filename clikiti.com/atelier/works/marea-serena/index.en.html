The route · 9 days

Pacific Coast and Galápagos

From Guayaquil to the islands, stopping where it’s worth it and resting whenever you need to. Each postcard tells you what you’ll do, how far you’ll walk and where you can sit down.

  • Departs from and returns to Guayaquil
  • 5 ports, 2 days on the open sea
  • Return flight included

Day 1 of 9

Guayaquil · boarding

  1. Day 1 Guayaquil

    Boarding with no lines and no stairs

    We pick you up at your hotel or the airport. You walk on board along a level gangway, and your luggage is already waiting in your cabin. Welcome dinner as we sail down the Guayas River.

    You’ll walk
    About 50 meters (160 ft). A wheelchair, if you’d like one.
    Rest stops
    A boarding lounge with armchairs and tea.

  2. Day 2 Manta

    The waterfront and the real “Panama” hat

    A stroll along the waterfront and a visit to a family workshop where toquilla straw hats, the original “Panama” hats, are woven by hand. Back on board for a nap.

    You’ll walk
    About 800 meters (half a mile), all flat and paved.
    Rest stops
    Shaded benches every 200 meters. Our van follows along.

  3. Day 3 Puerto López

    Whales, seen from the deck

    From June to October, humpback whales pass along this coast. We watch them from the ship, with a seat and binoculars for every guest. In March, the fishing village at dawn.

    You’ll walk
    Not at all, if you prefer: you can see everything from the deck.
    Rest stops
    Deck chairs and blankets at the bow.

  4. Days 4 and 5 Open sea

    Two days with only the sea in charge

    A thousand kilometers west, slowly. Talks about the islands and about Darwin, music in the afternoon, reading and long naps. No one sets your schedule but you.

    You’ll walk
    As much as you like: one lap of the deck is 180 meters (590 ft).
    Rest stops
    A library, a lounge with a view and hammocks in the shade.

  5. Days 6 and 7 Santa Cruz, Galápagos

    Giant tortoises, up close

    An accessible bus takes us up to the highlands, where giant tortoises graze in the wild. The next day, the breeding center in Puerto Ayora.

    You’ll walk
    About 600 meters (a third of a mile) on a firm path. An electric cart is available.
    Rest stops
    Benches at every lookout and a shaded shelter.

  6. Days 8 and 9 San Cristóbal and home

    Sea lions and Kicker Rock

    Sea lions sleep on the waterfront, just a few steps away. At sunset we sail past Kicker Rock, León Dormido to the locals. On the last day, a direct flight to Guayaquil, included.

    You’ll walk
    About 300 meters (1,000 ft) along the waterfront.
    Rest stops
    Shaded terraces facing the sea. Assistance at the airport.

On board the Serena

An ordinary day, told slowly

You can get to know a small ship in an afternoon. An elevator to all three decks, handrails in every corridor and not a single step between you and the sea.

07:00

Your cabin

You wake up to the sea in your window. Every cabin is ocean-view and roomy, and just a few steps from the elevator.

  • Step-free entrance and shower, with a fold-down seat.
  • Grab bars beside the bed, the toilet and the shower.
  • Soft floor-level lighting through the night.
  • A call button that alerts the nurse.

09:30

In the morning

Half an hour of gentle movement, standing or seated, at your own pace. Or, if you’d rather, watch the sea with a coffee.

  • A qualified physical therapist leads every class.
  • Non-slip flooring and an awning for shade.
  • Chairs with armrests if you’d rather stay seated.

13:00

At the table

Long, leisurely meals made with produce from the coast: ceviche, fish encocado in coconut sauce and fruit you may never have tasted.

  • Low-salt, sugar-free or gluten-free meals, without having to ask twice.
  • Large-print menus and good light at every table.
  • A shared table or a table for two: you choose, every day.

And when night falls, someone is always awake.

Around the clock

Always looked after

A doctor and two nurses travel with you. They know your medical history before you board, because you or your family talk it through with us, unhurried, over the phone.

  • A doctor and nurses 24 hours a day, at no extra cost.
  • Help with your medication, if you’d like it.
  • Oxygen and a defibrillator on every deck.
  • Coordinated medical transfer from any port.

For families

If you’re planning your parents’ trip, this letter is for you.

From Guayaquil

Dear family,

We know how this goes. You want your parents to see the Galápagos, and at the same time you wonder whether there will be stairs, whether a doctor will be close by, whether someone will notice when they need to sit down. Those are good questions. Here are our answers, in writing, so you can share them with your parents.

What the price includes

  • An accessible ocean-view cabin, for one or two people.
  • All meals, water, coffee and juice.
  • Gentle guided excursions, with accessible transport and rest stops.
  • A doctor and nurses on board 24 hours a day.
  • The return flight to Guayaquil and entry to Galápagos National Park.
  • Door-to-door transfers in Guayaquil, with help with your luggage.

Flights to Guayaquil and travel insurance are not included. We’ll explain everything over the phone, with no fine print.

How to book for them

  1. Call us, you or them.Tell us, in your own time, how well they walk, what medication they take and what they’re most looking forward to.
  2. We send you our proposal in writing.By email and on paper too, in large print, so you can read it together.
  3. A small deposit holds the booking.Cancellation is free up to 60 days before departure, for any reason.

P.S. If your parents would like, we’ll send you a short message with a photo every evening: where they are and how the day went.

Calmly, and with care,The Marea Serena team
