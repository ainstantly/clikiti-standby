**Answer:** Only 15% of the 189 independent landscaping, lawn care, hardscape and pool websites we scanned show any price: a visit, a package, a square foot or a project.

Clikiti scanned 189 independent landscaping, lawn care, hardscape and pool websites in October 2026 and checked each one against 12 checks: 8 questions homeowners ask before choosing, plus accessibility and speed checks tied to WCAG 2.2 and Core Web Vitals.

## Key findings

- Only 15% of the 189 independent landscaping, lawn care, hardscape and pool websites we scanned show any price: a visit, a package, a square foot or a project.
- A typical site answers five of the eight questions homeowners ask before choosing (mean 4.7).
- The median overall score is 79 out of 100 (mean 79).
- The least-answered questions are "Roughly what will it cost?" (15%), "Are you licensed and insured?" (29%) and "Do my neighbours vouch for you?" (44%).
- The best-answered are "Do you do what I need?" (95%) and "Can I see work you have done?" (86%).
- By kind of business, the highest median score is 81 (landscape design and build), against 80 for hardscape and outdoor living, 79 for lawn care and maintenance and 79 for pools and irrigation.
- 91% fail "Loads in under 2.5 s on a phone" (Core Web Vitals · LCP).
- 83% fail "Text contrast passes 4.5:1" (WCAG 1.4.3).
- 46% fail "Local business structured data" (Schema.org · LocalBusiness).

### 5 fixable problems to lead a pitch with

1. **Loads in under 2.5 s on a phone** (91% of sites, build). Fix: Resize and compress the hero image and gallery photos, defer scripts, aim for under 2.5 s on a phone. Pitch: "Your home page takes several seconds to appear on a homeowner's phone."
2. **Roughly what will it cost?** (85% of sites, content). Fix: Publish a starting price or a typical range for the common jobs: weekly mowing per visit, a cleanup, a patio per square foot, a pool opening. Pitch: ""Roughly what will it cost?" is the first question a homeowner has, and your site makes them wait for a site visit to hear it."
3. **Text contrast passes 4.5:1** (83% of sites, quick fix). Fix: Raise text contrast to at least 4.5:1 in the theme, especially text laid over garden photos. Pitch: "Parts of the site are hard to read, and it is a WCAG failure."
4. **Are you licensed and insured?** (71% of sites, quick fix). Fix: State "licensed and insured" with the licence or registration number in the footer, and name certifications (ICPI, Landscape Industry Certified, pesticide applicator). Pitch: "A homeowner letting a crew dig up the yard wants to know you are insured, and your site never says."
5. **Do my neighbours vouch for you?** (56% of sites, quick fix). Fix: Show recent reviews on the home page through a review widget or Review structured data, with the source named. Pitch: "Your neighbours vouch for you on Google, but nobody sees it on your own site."

### How to show a prospect their problems and a redesign

1. **Lead with their problem, not your services.** Open with one question the site fails, for example loads in under 2.5 s on a phone (91% of sites in this report miss it), and show the exact page where the answer should be.
2. **Put a number on it.** Compare their site with the sector: across 189 independent landscaping, lawn care, hardscape and pool companies sites, the median scores 79 out of 100 and answers 5 of the 8 questions homeowners ask. A number travels further inside the client than an opinion.
3. **Show the fix without days of spec work.** A rough redesign that answers those questions persuades better than a proposal, but it usually costs a designer a day or two per prospect. You can sketch it by hand, or use Clikiti: the scan is free and shows any site's problems in about a minute, and paid plans draft a redesign of the home page from the prospect's own brand, from about $2.33 per pitch.

## Method

- **Sample:** 189 independent landscaping, lawn care, hardscape and pool websites (48 landscape design and build, 49 lawn care and maintenance, 48 hardscape and outdoor living, 44 pools and irrigation).
- **When:** scanned October 2026; published 2026-10-15.
- **How:** the questions are read from the served HTML of each home page and the pages it links to. Load time, contrast and image descriptions come from Google Lighthouse (PageSpeed Insights) in a phone and a desktop render, with Chrome UX Report real-user data where it exists. A check passes only when the scan finds it; each check cites the standard it comes from.
- **Score:** Clikiti's overall website score (rubric v2): usability 30%, speed 25%, accessibility 25%, SEO 10%, trust 10%. It measures the website, not the independent landscaping, lawn care, hardscape and pool. The questions are reported separately and do not feed it.
- **Margin of error:** shares are exact for the sites scanned. Read as estimates for all independent landscaping, lawn care, hardscape and pool websites, they carry a margin of error of up to ±7.1 percentage points at 95% confidence (worst case, p = 0.5, n = 189).
- **Privacy:** results are published as aggregates or anonymously. Sites are listed by name in the appendix with no individual results; only the best-in-class example is named with its results.

## Appendix A: Methodology

189 websites, 4 types of organisation, 41 states, read on 2026-10-14 to 2026-10-15. Margin of error ±7.1 points at 95% confidence.

### A1. The research question

When a homeowner wants a new garden, a patio, a pool or someone to look after the lawn, and looks up an independent company online, usually on a phone, can they find out what it does, whether it works in their area, what its work looks like, roughly what it costs, whether they can ask for a quote without calling, whether it is licensed and insured, whether it can look after the yard every season and whether neighbours vouch for it?

Population: Independently owned US landscaping and outdoor-living companies with one to three locations and their own website, in four groups: landscape design and build, lawn care and maintenance, hardscape and outdoor living, and pools and irrigation. These are the outdoor businesses a small or mid-size web agency can realistically win as clients.

### A2. Who was studied

| Source | What it contributes | Sites |
|---|---|---:|
| Local search, landscape design and build | Independent residential landscape design, installation and design-build firms found in local search results, city by city | 56 |
| Local search, lawn care and maintenance | Independent lawn care, mowing, fertilisation and grounds maintenance companies found in local search results, city by city | 56 |
| Local search, hardscape and outdoor living | Independent patio, paver, retaining wall, stonework and outdoor kitchen contractors found in local search results, city by city | 47 |
| Local search, pools and irrigation | Independent pool builders, pool service companies and sprinkler and irrigation contractors found in local search results, city by city | 45 |

Included:
- An independently owned business with one to three locations, all in one state, and its own website (not a directory, marketplace or social profile)
- Serves homeowners: residential work is a main line of business, not a side to commercial, HOA or municipal contracting
- Based in the United States
- Home page responds and shows the business it claims to be (each home page title was checked before scanning)

Excluded:
- Franchises and national brands (for example Weed Man, TruGreen, Lawn Doctor, The Grounds Guys, US Lawns, NaturaLawn, Spring-Green, BrightView, Yellowstone Landscape, Davey Tree, Bartlett Tree Experts, Blue Haven Pools, Premier Pools & Spas, Conserva Irrigation) and any business with four or more locations or offices in more than one state
- Contractors whose work is mostly commercial, HOA, institutional or municipal
- Garden centres and nurseries whose site is a store catalogue, even when they also install
- Directories, marketplaces and lead-generation pages (Angi, HomeAdvisor, Thumbtack, Houzz, Yelp, Porch, LawnStarter, Lawn Love) and generic keyword domains that hide the business
- Parked, placeholder, under-maintenance, password-protected or unreachable sites, hijacked domains and duplicates

### A3. How the sites were read

- Pages: The home page, then for each of nine topics (services, service area, gallery or portfolio, prices, maintenance plans, about, reviews, estimate or quote, contact) the best-matching link on the home page.
- Speed and accessibility: Google PageSpeed Insights (Lighthouse) on a mid-range phone profile and on desktop, plus Chrome UX Report data from real visitors over the previous 28 days where Google publishes it for the site.
- Crawler conduct: One request at a time to each site, at least 400 ms apart, at most ten pages per site; robots.txt is read and obeyed for every page requested; a site that refuses is left out and never retried. User agent: `Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; ClikitiResearch/1.0; +https://clikiti.com)`.

### A5. The website score

Clikiti website score, rubric 2.0.0: a weighted mean of usability 30%, speed 25%, accessibility 25%, search 10% and trust 10%. It measures the website, not the organisation; the questions above do not feed it. Report-card letters: A 90+, B 75+, C 45+, D 30+, F below 30.

### A6. Precision

±7.1 points overall at 95% confidence (worst case p = 0.5, 1.96 × √(0.25/n)); by type: landscape design and build ±14.1 (n = 48); lawn care and maintenance ±14 (n = 49); hardscape and outdoor living ±14.1 (n = 48); pools and irrigation ±14.8 (n = 44).

### A7. Quality control

- Every home page in the cohort was fetched and its title checked against the listed business before scanning, and every home page was checked for gambling, pharmacy, adult and crypto spam
- Businesses were checked for duplicates across domains by phone number
- Detection rules were checked against the evidence they record for a sample of passes and fails for every rule, and revised before the final run
- Every published figure is recomputed from the data at build time, and a statement in the text that the data does not support stops the build
- Results are published only after automated checks for missing data, anonymity, accessibility of the report itself and the margin of error statement pass

### A8. Limitations

- A point-in-time snapshot: sites change, and a site may have improved or declined since it was read. Landscaping is seasonal, and the scan ran in October, when some companies promote fall cleanups, snow removal or winterisation rather than spring work.
- Only public pages were read: the home page and pages it links to. Chat widgets, multi-step quote forms, design tools and customer portals were not tested.
- Answers are read from the page as a server delivers it. Content that only appears after scripts run in the browser (some whole sites, galleries and review widgets) is not seen, which the study counts as a gap a search engine or a slow phone would also meet.
- The study reports what a website shows. It does not judge any company's design, planting, workmanship or prices, and it does not check whether any licence, certification or insurance is valid or current.
- Automated accessibility tests find only part of what WCAG covers; keyboard and screen-reader use need a manual audit.
- The sample is a structured list of real, independent businesses, not a random draw. Margins of error are given as a guide to precision, not as a guarantee that the results hold for every business in the country.
