    Method in brief

    How the sites were read

    Step 1 · all 170 sites

    Read

    The home page and up to nine pages it links to (products, carriers, quote, claims, team, about, client service, reviews, contact), read as a server delivers them. The client questions are answered here, by fixed published rules (Appendix A4).

    Step 2 · all 170 sites

    Measure

    Google Lighthouse (PageSpeed Insights) in a phone and a desktop render, with Chrome UX Report field data where it exists: load time, contrast and image descriptions. Repeatable.

    Step 3 · all 170 sites, score only

    Review

    An expert usability review of each home page against Nielsen's heuristics, used only in the usability part of the score. Every issue must name the heuristic, a severity and the element, or it is dropped.

    ScopeThis study examines how independent agencies present themselves on the web, not the quality of their advice, the cover they place or the premiums they quote.

    A1. The research question

    When someone shopping for cover, or a policyholder after a loss, looks an independent insurance agency up online, usually on a phone, can they find out how to reach it and when it is open, what it can insure, which insurance companies it represents, whether they can start a quote online, how to file a claim, who their agent would be and whether that agent is licensed, whether they can pay a bill or change a policy without calling, and whether other clients vouch for it?

    Population. Independently owned US insurance agencies with one to three offices and their own website, representing more than one insurance company, in four groups: personal lines (home and auto), commercial and business, life, health and Medicare, and specialty (farm and crop, marine, flood and coastal). These are the insurance businesses a small or mid-size web agency can realistically win as clients.

    A2. Who was studied

    The sample was built from local search listings, city by city, then screened so that every site in it is one an independent agency could realistically win.

    Included

    • An independently owned agency with one to three offices and its own website (not a directory, marketplace or social profile)
    • Represents more than one insurance company
    • Based in the United States
    • Home page responds and shows the agency it claims to be (each home page title was checked before scanning)

    Excluded

    • Captive agents of a single carrier, whose pages the carrier hosts or controls (for example State Farm, Allstate, Farmers, Nationwide, American Family, Country Financial and Farm Bureau agent pages)
    • Insurance companies themselves
    • National brokers and agencies owned by a broker roll-up (for example Marsh, Aon, Gallagher, Brown & Brown, HUB, Acrisure, Alera, AssuredPartners, World and Patriot Growth), identified from the site's own ownership statement; membership of an independent network such as SIAA or Iroquois does not exclude an agency
    • Franchises (for example Goosehead, Brightway, Fiesta, Estrella) and any agency with four or more offices
    • Quote comparison, lead-generation and directory sites
    • Parked, placeholder, password-protected, hijacked or unreachable sites, and duplicates

    A3. How the sites were read

    Pages. The home page, then for each of nine topics (products, carriers, quote, claims, team, about, client service, reviews, contact) the best-matching link on the home page.

    Speed and accessibility. Google PageSpeed Insights (Lighthouse) on a mid-range phone profile and on desktop, plus Chrome UX Report data from real visitors over the previous 28 days where Google publishes it for the site.

    Crawler conduct. One request at a time to each site, at least 400 ms apart, at most ten pages per site; robots.txt is read and obeyed for every page requested; a site that refuses is left out and never retried. The crawler identifies itself as Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; ClikitiResearch/1.0; +https://clikiti.com).

    A5. The website score

    Each site's overall score (0 to 100) is Clikiti's website score, rubric 2.0.0. It measures the website, not the agency, and the client questions above do not feed it. It is a weighted mean of five pillars:

    PillarWeightBased on
    Usability30%Checks of the home page for broken links and images, and an expert review of the page as rendered on a phone and on a desktop screen, against Jakob Nielsen's ten usability heuristics. Each issue must name the heuristic, a severity on the Nielsen Norman Group scale and the exact element or wording; issues without all three are discarded, and two independent passes must agree.
    Speed25%Google Lighthouse on a mid-range phone (60%) and on desktop (20%), and real-visitor Core Web Vitals from the Chrome UX Report (20%) where available.
    Accessibility25%Google Lighthouse accessibility audits (70%) and Clikiti's own WCAG checks of the served page (30%).
    Search10%Google Lighthouse SEO audits (60%) and Clikiti's own search checks of the served page (40%).
    Trust10%Google Lighthouse best-practice audits (40%) and security header checks (60%).

    Within a pillar, each issue costs points by severity (critical, high, medium, low) and by how many times it occurs; issues based on judgement count at 70% of measured ones, and duplicates of the same root cause count once. On the four agency profiles, the number out of 100 is this overall score, and each dot is one check in its group: filled when the site passes it, hollow when it does not.

    A6. Precision

    With 170 sites, a share reported for the whole sample is precise to within ±7.5 percentage points at 95% confidence (worst case, a 50% share; 1.96 × √(0.25/n)). By type: personal lines ±13 (n = 57); commercial and business ±14.9 (n = 43); life, health and medicare ±15.3 (n = 41); specialty: farm, marine and flood ±18.2 (n = 29). Differences between types smaller than these margins should be read as similar. Percentages are rounded to whole numbers; medians are the middle value of the sites in a group.

    A7. Quality control

    • Every home page in the cohort was fetched and its title checked against the listed agency before scanning, and every home page was checked for unrelated spam content (gambling, pharmacy, adult, crypto)
    • Ownership statements in site footers were checked for broker roll-ups before scanning
    • Detection rules were checked against the evidence they record for a sample of agencies in every group, and revised before the final run
    • The agency-website vendor behind each site, where its markup names one, is recorded so that template boilerplate can be told apart from an agency's own answers
    • Every published figure is recomputed from the data at build time, and a statement in the text that the data does not support stops the build
    • Results are published only after automated checks for missing data, anonymity, accessibility of the report itself and the margin of error statement pass

    A8. Limitations

    • A point-in-time snapshot: sites change, and a site may have improved or declined since it was read.
    • Only public pages were read: the home page and pages it links to. Quote forms were not submitted, client portals were not logged into and comparative raters were not run.
    • Answers are read from the page as a server delivers it. Content that only appears after scripts run in the browser (some quote widgets, review widgets and carrier lists) is not seen, which the study counts as a gap a search engine or a slow phone would also meet.
    • Many agency sites are built on a small number of agency-website platforms that supply standard pages; an answer that comes from such a template counts the same as one the agency wrote.
    • The study reports what a website shows. It does not judge any agency's advice, any policy's cover or any premium, or compliance with any state insurance licensing or advertising rule.
    • Automated accessibility tests find only part of what WCAG covers; keyboard and screen-reader use need a manual audit.
    • The sample is a structured list of real, independent agencies, not a random draw. Margins of error are given as a guide to precision, not as a guarantee that the results hold for every agency in the country.
