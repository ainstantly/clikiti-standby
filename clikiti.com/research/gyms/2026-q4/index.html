        05 · Method in brief

        How the sites were read

        Step 1 · all 165 sites

        Read

        The home page and up to twelve pages it links to (memberships and prices, free trial, schedule, coaches, beginners, policies, hours, location, reviews, about, contact), read as a server delivers them. The member questions are answered here, by fixed published rules (Appendix A4).

        Step 2 · all 165 sites

        Measure

        Google Lighthouse (PageSpeed Insights) in a phone and a desktop render, with Chrome UX Report field data where it exists: load time, contrast and image descriptions. Repeatable.

        Step 3 · all 165 sites, score only

        Review

        An expert usability review of each home page against Nielsen's heuristics, used only in the usability part of the score. Every issue must name the heuristic, a severity and the element, or it is dropped.

        ScopeThis study examines how independent gyms and fitness studios present themselves on the web, not the quality of their coaching, their equipment or their prices.

        5 problems to lead a pitch with

        1. 94%
          Loads in under 2.5 s on a phone Build

          Resize and compress the hero image and the class videos, defer the booking widget, aim for under 2.5 s on a phone.

          Your home page takes several seconds to appear on a phone in the car park.
        2. 80%
          Where do I park, and can I bring my kids? Quick fix

          One line on parking and one on childcare (or that there is none), on the contact or first-visit page.

          Parents and drivers want to know two practical things before the first class, and your site answers neither.
        3. 79%
          ExerciseGym, HealthClub or SportsActivityLocation structured data Quick fix

          Add ExerciseGym, HealthClub or SportsActivityLocation structured data with the address, phone and opening hours.

          Search engines and assistants have to guess where you are and when you are open.
        4. 76%
          Text contrast passes 4.5:1 Quick fix

          Raise text contrast to at least 4.5:1 in the theme, including text set over photographs.

          Grey text on dark photographs is hard to read, and it is a WCAG failure.
        5. 71%
          Who will coach me, and are they qualified? Content

          A coaches page with each coach named, a photograph and the certifications they hold (CrossFit Level 2, NASM, belt rank, RYT 200, a Pilates certification).

          Your coaches are qualified, and the site never says so. A first-timer wants to know who will be in the room.

        A1. The research question

        When someone wants to join a gym, start CrossFit, learn to box or try a yoga, pilates or cycling class, and looks an independent gym or studio up online, usually on a phone, can they find out what it costs, whether they can try it first, when the classes are, who will coach them and with what certification, where a beginner starts, how a membership ends, when the doors are open, where to park or whether there is childcare, and whether other members vouch for it?

        Population. Independently owned US gyms and fitness studios with one to three locations and their own website, in four groups: independent gyms, CrossFit and functional-fitness gyms, boxing and martial arts gyms, and boutique studios (yoga, pilates, cycling, barre). These are the fitness businesses a small or mid-size web agency can realistically win as clients.

        A3. How the sites were read

        Pages. The home page, then for each of nine topics (memberships and prices, free trial or intro offer, class schedule, coaches, beginners, policies and FAQ, about and facility, reviews, contact and hours) the best-matching link on the home page.

        Speed and accessibility. Google PageSpeed Insights (Lighthouse) on a mid-range phone profile and on desktop, plus Chrome UX Report data from real visitors over the previous 28 days where Google publishes it for the site.

        Crawler conduct. One request at a time to each site, at least 400 ms apart, at most ten pages per site; robots.txt is read and obeyed for every page requested; a site that refuses is left out and never retried. The crawler identifies itself as Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; ClikitiResearch/1.0; +https://clikiti.com).

        A5. The website score

        Each site's overall score (0 to 100) is Clikiti's website score, rubric 2.0.0. It measures the website, not the gym, and the member questions above do not feed it. It is a weighted mean of five pillars:

        PillarWeightBased on
        Usability30%Checks of the home page for broken links and images, and an expert review of the page as rendered on a phone and on a desktop screen, against Jakob Nielsen's ten usability heuristics. Each issue must name the heuristic, a severity on the Nielsen Norman Group scale and the exact element or wording; issues without all three are discarded, and two independent passes must agree.
        Speed25%Google Lighthouse on a mid-range phone (60%) and on desktop (20%), and real-visitor Core Web Vitals from the Chrome UX Report (20%) where available.
        Accessibility25%Google Lighthouse accessibility audits (70%) and Clikiti's own WCAG checks of the served page (30%).
        Search10%Google Lighthouse SEO audits (60%) and Clikiti's own search checks of the served page (40%).
        Trust10%Google Lighthouse best-practice audits (40%) and security header checks (60%).

        Within a pillar, each issue costs points by severity (critical, high, medium, low) and by how many times it occurs; issues based on judgement count at 70% of measured ones, and duplicates of the same root cause count once. On the four gym profiles, the number out of 100 is this overall score, and each dot is one check in its group: filled when the site passes it, hollow when it does not.

        A6. Precision

        With 165 sites, a share reported for the whole sample is precise to within ±7.6 percentage points at 95% confidence (worst case, a 50% share; 1.96 × √(0.25/n)). By type: independent gyms ±15.7 (n = 39); crossfit and functional fitness ±16.3 (n = 36); boxing and martial arts ±14.3 (n = 47); boutique studios ±14.9 (n = 43). Differences between types smaller than these margins should be read as similar. Percentages are rounded to whole numbers; medians are the middle value of the sites in a group.

        A7. Quality control

        • Every home page in the cohort was fetched and its title checked against the listed gym before scanning
        • Every home page was checked for hijacked content (gambling, pharmacy, adult and crypto spam) before scanning
        • Every home page was checked for a second website of the same gym (shared phone numbers and names)
        • Detection rules were checked against the evidence they record for a sample of gyms in every group, and revised before the final run
        • Every published figure is recomputed from the data at build time, and a statement in the text that the data does not support stops the build
        • Results are published only after automated checks for missing data, anonymity, accessibility of the report itself and the margin of error statement pass

        A8. Limitations

        • A point-in-time snapshot: sites change, and a site may have improved or declined since it was read.
        • Only public pages were read: the home page and pages it links to. Member portals, apps, online stores and multi-step booking flows were not tested.
        • Answers are read from the page as a server delivers it. Content that only appears after scripts run in the browser (some schedulers and review widgets) is not seen, which the study counts as a gap a search engine or a slow phone would also meet.
        • The study reports what a website shows. It does not judge any gym's coaching, equipment or prices, and it is not fitness or medical advice.
        • Automated accessibility tests find only part of what WCAG covers; keyboard and screen-reader use need a manual audit.
        • The sample is a structured list of real, independent gyms and studios, not a random draw. Margins of error are given as a guide to precision, not as a guarantee that the results hold for every gym in the country.

        About this study
