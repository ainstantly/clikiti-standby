# The State of Independent Gym Websites 2026 (Q4 2026)

**Answer:** Only 20% of the 165 independent gym and fitness studio websites we scanned say where to park or whether there is childcare.

*Free to use and cite under CC BY 4.0. Credit Clikiti with a link.* For small agencies and freelancers who build websites for independent gyms, CrossFit boxes, fight gyms and boutique studios. We scanned 165 of their websites: 0% score under 60 out of 100, and 91% leave at least three of the questions would-be members ask unanswered. Here is where the winnable work is, which problems to lead a pitch with, and how to show them without days of spec design.

Clikiti scanned 165 independent gym and fitness studio websites in October 2026 and checked each one against 13 checks: 9 questions would-be members ask before choosing, plus accessibility and speed checks tied to WCAG 2.2 and Core Web Vitals.

## Key findings

- Only 20% of the 165 independent gym and fitness studio websites we scanned say where to park or whether there is childcare.
- A typical site answers four of the nine questions would-be members ask before choosing (mean 3.8).
- The median overall score is 80 out of 100 (mean 79).
- The least-answered questions are "Where do I park, and can I bring my kids?" (20%), "Who will coach me, and are they qualified?" (29%) and "Do other members vouch for it?" (31%).
- The best-answered are "Can I try it first?" (67%) and "What does it cost to join?" (64%).
- By kind of business, the highest median score is 80 (independent gyms), against 80 for boutique studios, 79 for CrossFit and functional fitness and 78 for boxing and martial arts.
- 94% fail "Loads in under 2.5 s on a phone" (Core Web Vitals · LCP).
- 79% fail "ExerciseGym, HealthClub or SportsActivityLocation structured data" (Schema.org · ExerciseGym).
- 76% fail "Text contrast passes 4.5:1" (WCAG 1.4.3).

### 5 fixable problems to lead a pitch with

1. **Loads in under 2.5 s on a phone** (94% of sites, build). Fix: Resize and compress the hero image and the class videos, defer the booking widget, aim for under 2.5 s on a phone. Pitch: "Your home page takes several seconds to appear on a phone in the car park."
2. **Where do I park, and can I bring my kids?** (80% of sites, quick fix). Fix: One line on parking and one on childcare (or that there is none), on the contact or first-visit page. Pitch: "Parents and drivers want to know two practical things before the first class, and your site answers neither."
3. **ExerciseGym, HealthClub or SportsActivityLocation structured data** (79% of sites, quick fix). Fix: Add ExerciseGym, HealthClub or SportsActivityLocation structured data with the address, phone and opening hours. Pitch: "Search engines and assistants have to guess where you are and when you are open."
4. **Text contrast passes 4.5:1** (76% of sites, quick fix). Fix: Raise text contrast to at least 4.5:1 in the theme, including text set over photographs. Pitch: "Grey text on dark photographs is hard to read, and it is a WCAG failure."
5. **Who will coach me, and are they qualified?** (71% of sites, content). Fix: A coaches page with each coach named, a photograph and the certifications they hold (CrossFit Level 2, NASM, belt rank, RYT 200, a Pilates certification). Pitch: "Your coaches are qualified, and the site never says so. A first-timer wants to know who will be in the room."

### How to show a prospect their problems and a redesign

1. **Lead with their problem, not your services.** Open with one question the site fails, for example loads in under 2.5 s on a phone (94% of sites in this report miss it), and show the exact page where the answer should be.
2. **Put a number on it.** Compare their site with the sector: across 165 independent gyms, CrossFit boxes, boxing and martial arts gyms and boutique studios sites, the median scores 80 out of 100 and answers 4 of the 9 questions would-be members ask. A number travels further inside the client than an opinion.
3. **Show the fix without days of spec work.** A rough redesign that answers those questions persuades better than a proposal, but it usually costs a designer a day or two per prospect. You can sketch it by hand, or use Clikiti: the scan is free and shows any site's problems in about a minute, and paid plans draft a redesign of the home page from the prospect's own brand, from about $2.33 per pitch.

## Method

- **Sample:** 165 independent gym and fitness studio websites (39 independent gyms, 36 crossfit and functional fitness, 47 boxing and martial arts, 43 boutique studios).
- **When:** scanned October 2026; published 2026-10-15.
- **How:** the questions are read from the served HTML of each home page and the pages it links to. Load time, contrast and image descriptions come from Google Lighthouse (PageSpeed Insights) in a phone and a desktop render, with Chrome UX Report real-user data where it exists. A check passes only when the scan finds it; each check cites the standard it comes from.
- **Score:** Clikiti's overall website score (rubric v2): usability 30%, speed 25%, accessibility 25%, SEO 10%, trust 10%. It measures the website, not the independent gym and fitness studio. The questions are reported separately and do not feed it.
- **Margin of error:** shares are exact for the sites scanned. Read as estimates for all independent gym and fitness studio websites, they carry a margin of error of up to ±7.6 percentage points at 95% confidence (worst case, p = 0.5, n = 165).
- **Privacy:** results are published as aggregates or anonymously. Sites are listed by name in the appendix with no individual results; only the best-in-class example is named with its results.

## Appendix A: Methodology

165 websites, 4 types of organisation, 48 states, read on 2026-10-15. Margin of error ±7.6 points at 95% confidence.

### A1. The research question

When someone wants to join a gym, start CrossFit, learn to box or try a yoga, pilates or cycling class, and looks an independent gym or studio up online, usually on a phone, can they find out what it costs, whether they can try it first, when the classes are, who will coach them and with what certification, where a beginner starts, how a membership ends, when the doors are open, where to park or whether there is childcare, and whether other members vouch for it?

Population: Independently owned US gyms and fitness studios with one to three locations and their own website, in four groups: independent gyms, CrossFit and functional-fitness gyms, boxing and martial arts gyms, and boutique studios (yoga, pilates, cycling, barre). These are the fitness businesses a small or mid-size web agency can realistically win as clients.

### A3. How the sites were read

- Pages: The home page, then for each of nine topics (memberships and prices, free trial or intro offer, class schedule, coaches, beginners, policies and FAQ, about and facility, reviews, contact and hours) the best-matching link on the home page.
- Speed and accessibility: Google PageSpeed Insights (Lighthouse) on a mid-range phone profile and on desktop, plus Chrome UX Report data from real visitors over the previous 28 days where Google publishes it for the site.
- Crawler conduct: One request at a time to each site, at least 400 ms apart, at most ten pages per site; robots.txt is read and obeyed for every page requested; a site that refuses is left out and never retried. User agent: `Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; ClikitiResearch/1.0; +https://clikiti.com)`.

### A5. The website score

Clikiti website score, rubric 2.0.0: a weighted mean of usability 30%, speed 25%, accessibility 25%, search 10% and trust 10%. It measures the website, not the organisation; the questions above do not feed it. Report-card letters: A 90+, B 75+, C 45+, D 30+, F below 30.

### A6. Precision

±7.6 points overall at 95% confidence (worst case p = 0.5, 1.96 × √(0.25/n)); by type: independent gyms ±15.7 (n = 39); crossfit and functional fitness ±16.3 (n = 36); boxing and martial arts ±14.3 (n = 47); boutique studios ±14.9 (n = 43).

### A7. Quality control

- Every home page in the cohort was fetched and its title checked against the listed gym before scanning
- Every home page was checked for hijacked content (gambling, pharmacy, adult and crypto spam) before scanning
- Every home page was checked for a second website of the same gym (shared phone numbers and names)
- Detection rules were checked against the evidence they record for a sample of gyms in every group, and revised before the final run
- Every published figure is recomputed from the data at build time, and a statement in the text that the data does not support stops the build
- Results are published only after automated checks for missing data, anonymity, accessibility of the report itself and the margin of error statement pass

### A8. Limitations

- A point-in-time snapshot: sites change, and a site may have improved or declined since it was read.
- Only public pages were read: the home page and pages it links to. Member portals, apps, online stores and multi-step booking flows were not tested.
- Answers are read from the page as a server delivers it. Content that only appears after scripts run in the browser (some schedulers and review widgets) is not seen, which the study counts as a gap a search engine or a slow phone would also meet.
- The study reports what a website shows. It does not judge any gym's coaching, equipment or prices, and it is not fitness or medical advice.
- Automated accessibility tests find only part of what WCAG covers; keyboard and screen-reader use need a manual audit.
- The sample is a structured list of real, independent gyms and studios, not a random draw. Margins of error are given as a guide to precision, not as a guarantee that the results hold for every gym in the country.
