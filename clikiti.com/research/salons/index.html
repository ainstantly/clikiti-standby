        05 · Method in brief

        How the sites were read

        Step 1 · all 193 sites

        Read

        The home page and up to twelve pages it links to (services and prices, booking, team, gallery, products, policies, about, reviews, contact, accessibility), read as a server delivers them. The client questions are answered here, by fixed published rules (Appendix A4).

        Step 2 · all 193 sites

        Measure

        Google Lighthouse (PageSpeed Insights) in a phone and a desktop render, with Chrome UX Report field data where it exists: load time, contrast and image descriptions. Repeatable.

        Step 3 · all 193 sites, score only

        Review

        An expert usability review of each home page against Nielsen's heuristics, used only in the usability part of the score. Every issue must name the heuristic, a severity and the element, or it is dropped.

        ScopeThis study examines how independent salons and barbershops present themselves on the web, not the quality of their cuts, colour, products or prices.

        5 problems to lead a pitch with

        1. 97%
          Can I get in with a wheelchair? Quick fix

          One plain line on the contact page: step-free entrance or not, the floor, an elevator, accessible parking, a shampoo station that suits a wheelchair.

          A client who uses a wheelchair cannot tell from your site whether they can get through the door.
        2. 84%
          Loads in under 2.5 s on a phone Build

          Resize and compress the hero image and gallery photos, defer the booking and Instagram scripts, aim for under 2.5 s on a phone.

          Your home page takes several seconds to appear on a client’s phone.
        3. 84%
          HairSalon, BarberShop or BeautySalon structured data Quick fix

          Add HairSalon or BarberShop structured data with the name, address, phone, opening hours and booking link.

          Search engines and assistants have to guess when you are open and how to book.
        4. 82%
          Do other clients vouch for you? Quick fix

          Show recent client reviews on the home page through a review widget or Review structured data, with the source named.

          Your clients praise you on Google every week, and none of it is on your own site.
        5. 80%
          Can I just walk in? Quick fix

          One line beside the hours: walk-ins welcome, walk-ins when a chair is free, or by appointment only.

          A client standing outside wants to know whether to walk in or book, and your site does not say.

        A1. The research question

        When someone wants a new hair salon or barber and looks one up online, usually on a phone, can they find out what a cut or colour costs, whether they can book a chosen stylist online, whose work they would be getting, what happens if they cancel, whether they can walk in, when the salon is open, which products it uses, whether they can get in with a wheelchair, and whether other clients vouch for it?

        Population. Independently owned US hair salons and barbershops with one to three locations and their own website, in four groups: hair salons, barbershops, colour and specialty salons (colour and balayage specialists, curly, extensions and textured hair), and hair salons with spa services. These are the salons a small or mid-size web agency can realistically win as clients.

        A3. How the sites were read

        Pages. The home page, then for each of twelve topics (services, prices, booking, team, gallery and portfolio, products, policies and FAQ, about, reviews, spa services, contact, accessibility) the best-matching link on the home page.

        Speed and accessibility. Google PageSpeed Insights (Lighthouse) on a mid-range phone profile and on desktop, plus Chrome UX Report data from real visitors over the previous 28 days where Google publishes it for the site.

        Crawler conduct. One request at a time to each site, at least 400 ms apart, at most thirteen pages per site; robots.txt is read and obeyed for every page requested; a site that refuses is left out and never retried. The crawler identifies itself as Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; ClikitiResearch/1.0; +https://clikiti.com).

        A5. The website score

        Each site's overall score (0 to 100) is Clikiti's website score, rubric 2.0.0. It measures the website, not the salon, and the client questions above do not feed it. It is a weighted mean of five pillars:

        PillarWeightBased on
        Usability30%Checks of the home page for broken links and images, and an expert review of the page as rendered on a phone and on a desktop screen, against Jakob Nielsen's ten usability heuristics. Each issue must name the heuristic, a severity on the Nielsen Norman Group scale and the exact element or wording; issues without all three are discarded, and two independent passes must agree.
        Speed25%Google Lighthouse on a mid-range phone (60%) and on desktop (20%), and real-visitor Core Web Vitals from the Chrome UX Report (20%) where available.
        Accessibility25%Google Lighthouse accessibility audits (70%) and Clikiti's own WCAG checks of the served page (30%).
        Search10%Google Lighthouse SEO audits (60%) and Clikiti's own search checks of the served page (40%).
        Trust10%Google Lighthouse best-practice audits (40%) and security header checks (60%).

        Within a pillar, each issue costs points by severity (critical, high, medium, low) and by how many times it occurs; issues based on judgement count at 70% of measured ones, and duplicates of the same root cause count once. On the four salon profiles, the number out of 100 is this overall score, and each dot is one check in its group: filled when the site passes it, hollow when it does not.

        A6. Precision

        With 193 sites, a share reported for the whole sample is precise to within ±7.1 percentage points at 95% confidence (worst case, a 50% share; 1.96 × √(0.25/n)). By type: hair salons ±13.5 (n = 53); barbershops ±13.7 (n = 51); colour and specialty salons ±14.3 (n = 47); salons with spa services ±15.1 (n = 42). Differences between types smaller than these margins should be read as similar. Percentages are rounded to whole numbers; medians are the middle value of the sites in a group.

        A7. Quality control

        • Every home page in the cohort was fetched and its title checked against the listed salon before scanning
        • Every home page was checked for hijacked content (gambling, pharmacy, adult and crypto spam) before scanning
        • Every home page was checked for a second website of the same salon (shared phone numbers and names)
        • Detection rules were checked against the evidence they record: seven passing and six failing salons per question, read by hand over seven rounds (rules v1 to v7), and revised before the final run
        • Every published figure is recomputed from the data at build time, and a statement in the text that the data does not support stops the build
        • Results are published only after automated checks for missing data, anonymity, accessibility of the report itself and the margin of error statement pass

        A8. Limitations

        • A point-in-time snapshot: sites change, and a site may have improved or declined since it was read.
        • Only public pages were read: the home page and pages it links to. Booking flows inside booking apps were not completed, so the study records that a site offers booking with a chosen stylist, not how well that flow works.
        • Answers are read from the page as a server delivers it. Content that only appears after scripts run in the browser (some booking widgets, Instagram feeds and review widgets) is not seen, which the study counts as a gap a search engine or a slow phone would also meet.
        • 17 candidates blocked automated visitors, served them a bot challenge or did not respond, and are not in the study; sites behind such protection may differ from those studied.
        • The study reports what a website shows. It does not judge any salon's haircuts, colour work, products, hygiene or prices.
        • Automated accessibility tests find only part of what WCAG covers; keyboard and screen-reader use need a manual audit.
        • The sample is a structured list of real, independent salons and barbershops, not a random draw. Margins of error are given as a guide to precision, not as a guarantee that the results hold for every salon in the country.
