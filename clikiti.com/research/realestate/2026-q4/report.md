# The State of Independent Real Estate Websites 2026 (Q4 2026)

**Answer:** Only 13% of the 133 independent real estate websites we scanned say what they charge.

*Free to use and cite under CC BY 4.0. Credit Clikiti with a link.* For small agencies and freelancers who sell websites to independent brokerages, agent teams and property managers. We scanned 133 of their websites: 2% score under 60 out of 100, and 86% leave at least three of the questions buyers, sellers and landlords ask unanswered. Here is where the winnable work is, which problems to lead a pitch with, and how to show them without days of spec design.

Clikiti scanned 133 independent real estate websites in October 2026 and checked each one against 12 checks: 8 questions buyers, sellers and landlords ask before choosing, plus accessibility and speed checks tied to WCAG 2.2 and Core Web Vitals.

## Key findings

- Only 13% of the 133 independent real estate websites we scanned say what they charge.
- A typical site answers three of the eight questions buyers, sellers and landlords ask before choosing (mean 3.3).
- The median overall score is 75 out of 100 (mean 75).
- The least-answered questions are "What will it cost me?" (13%), "Are you licensed?" (20%) and "Have you sold or managed homes like mine?" (26%).
- The best-answered are "Can I see what you have for sale or rent?" (76%) and "How do I reach you or book a showing?" (60%).
- By kind of business, the highest median score is 79 (independent property managers), against 74 for independent residential brokerages, 74 for solo agents and small teams and 72 for luxury boutiques.
- 87% fail "Text contrast passes 4.5:1" (WCAG 1.4.3).
- 78% fail "RealEstateAgent or listing structured data" (Schema.org · RealEstateAgent).
- 77% fail "Loads in under 2.5 s on a phone" (Core Web Vitals · LCP).

### 5 fixable problems to lead a pitch with

1. **What will it cost me?** (87% of sites, content). Fix: Say plainly how the firm is paid: commission or flat fee and what it covers, or the management and leasing fees. Pitch: "Since buyer agreements became the norm, clients ask what you charge first, and your site does not say."
2. **Text contrast passes 4.5:1** (87% of sites, quick fix). Fix: Raise text contrast to at least 4.5:1, especially text laid over hero photos. Pitch: "Parts of the site are hard to read, and it is a WCAG failure."
3. **Are you licensed?** (80% of sites, quick fix). Fix: Show the broker of record and each agent’s licence number in the footer and on agent pages. Pitch: "A careful client checks the licence first, and your site makes them look it up elsewhere."
4. **RealEstateAgent or listing structured data** (78% of sites, quick fix). Fix: Add RealEstateAgent structured data with the firm’s name, address, phone and areas served. Pitch: "Search engines and assistants have to guess who you are and where you work."
5. **Loads in under 2.5 s on a phone** (77% of sites, build). Fix: Resize and compress the hero photo, defer the listing-search scripts, aim for under 2.5 s on a phone. Pitch: "Your home page takes several seconds to appear on a buyer’s phone at the curb."

### How to show a prospect their problems and a redesign

1. **Lead with their problem, not your services.** Open with one question the site fails, for example "What will it cost me?" (87% of sites in this report miss it), and show the exact page where the answer should be.
2. **Put a number on it.** Compare their site with the sector: across 133 independent brokerages, agent teams and property managers sites, the median scores 75 out of 100 and answers 3 of the 8 questions buyers, sellers and landlords ask. A number travels further inside the client than an opinion.
3. **Show the fix without days of spec work.** A rough redesign that answers those questions persuades better than a proposal, but it usually costs a designer a day or two per prospect. You can sketch it by hand, or use Clikiti: the scan is free and shows any site's problems in about a minute, and paid plans draft a redesign of the home page from the prospect's own brand, from about $2.33 per pitch.

## Method

- **Sample:** 133 independent real estate websites (46 independent residential brokerages, 22 solo agents and small teams, 47 independent property managers, 18 luxury boutiques).
- **When:** scanned October 2026; published 2026-10-15.
- **How:** the questions are read from the served HTML of each home page and the pages it links to. Load time, contrast and image descriptions come from Google Lighthouse (PageSpeed Insights) in a phone and a desktop render, with Chrome UX Report real-user data where it exists. A check passes only when the scan finds it; each check cites the standard it comes from.
- **Score:** Clikiti's overall website score (rubric v2): usability 30%, speed 25%, accessibility 25%, SEO 10%, trust 10%. It measures the website, not the independent real estate. The questions are reported separately and do not feed it.
- **Margin of error:** shares are exact for the sites scanned. Read as estimates for all independent real estate websites, they carry a margin of error of up to ±8.5 percentage points at 95% confidence (worst case, p = 0.5, n = 133).
- **Privacy:** results are published as aggregates or anonymously. Sites are listed by name in the appendix with no individual results; only the best-in-class example is named with its results.

## Appendix A: Methodology

133 websites, 4 types of organisation, 36 states, read on 2026-10-14 to 2026-10-15. Margin of error ±8.5 points at 95% confidence.

### A1. The research question

When someone wants to buy, sell or rent out a home and looks up an independent agent, brokerage or property manager online, usually on a phone, can they search what is for sale or rent, reach the business or ask for a showing, tell whether it works in their area, get a home valuation or rental analysis, see a track record, check a licence number, find out what it charges and see whether past clients vouch for it?

Population: Independently owned US residential real estate businesses with their own website, in four groups: independent residential brokerages, solo agents and small teams, independent property managers, and luxury boutiques. These are the real estate businesses a small or mid-size web agency can realistically win as clients.

### A2. Who was studied

| Source | What it contributes | Sites |
|---|---|---:|
| Local search, independent brokerages | Independently owned residential brokerages found in local search results and brokerage directories, city by city | 85 |
| Local search, agents and teams | Solo agents and small teams with their own website, found in local search results, city by city | 25 |
| Local search, property managers | Independently owned residential property management companies found in local search results, city by city | 45 |
| Local search, luxury boutiques | Independent brokerages and teams that market themselves as luxury or fine homes specialists, found in local search results, city by city | 17 |

Included:
- An independently owned brokerage, team or property manager with its own website (not a directory, portal or social profile)
- Based in the United States

### A3. How the sites were read

- Pages: The home page, then for each of nine topics (listings or rentals, home valuation, sold or track record, agents or team, about, reviews, areas or communities, fees or services, contact) the best-matching link on the home page.
- Speed and accessibility: Google PageSpeed Insights (Lighthouse) on a mid-range phone profile and on desktop, plus Chrome UX Report data from real visitors over the previous 28 days where Google publishes it for the site.
- Crawler conduct: One request at a time to each site, at least 400 ms apart, at most ten pages per site; robots.txt is read and obeyed for every page requested; a site that refuses is left out and never retried. User agent: `Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; ClikitiResearch/1.0; +https://clikiti.com)`.

### A5. The website score

Clikiti website score, rubric 2.0.0: a weighted mean of usability 30%, speed 25%, accessibility 25%, search 10% and trust 10%. It measures the website, not the organisation; the questions above do not feed it. Report-card letters: A 90+, B 75+, C 45+, D 30+, F below 30.

### A6. Precision

±8.5 points overall at 95% confidence (worst case p = 0.5, 1.96 × √(0.25/n)); by type: independent residential brokerages ±14.4 (n = 46); solo agents and small teams ±20.9 (n = 22); independent property managers ±14.3 (n = 47); luxury boutiques ±23.1 (n = 18).

### A7. Quality control

- Every home page in the cohort was fetched and its title checked against the listed business before scanning
- Every home page was checked for gambling, pharmacy, adult and crypto spam (hijacked domains) before scanning
- Detection rules were checked against the evidence they record for a sample of businesses in every group, and revised before the final run
- Every published figure is recomputed from the data at build time, and a statement in the text that the data does not support stops the build
- Results are published only after automated checks for missing data, anonymity, accessibility of the report itself and the margin of error statement pass

### A8. Limitations

- A point-in-time snapshot: sites change, and a site may have improved or declined since it was read.
- Only public pages were read: the home page and pages it links to. Listing-search tools, saved searches, chat widgets and multi-step valuation forms were not tested.
- Answers are read from the page as a server delivers it. Content that only appears after scripts run in the browser (many listing searches, valuation tools and review widgets) is not seen unless the page links to it or loads it by name, which the study counts as a gap a search engine or a slow phone would also meet.
- Licence-number display rules differ by state; the study reports whether a number is shown, not whether a business meets its state's advertising rules.
- The study reports what a website shows. It does not judge any agent, brokerage, property manager, listing or price, or compliance with any licensing, advertising or fair housing rule.
- Automated accessibility tests find only part of what WCAG covers; keyboard and screen-reader use need a manual audit.
- The sample is a structured list of real, independent businesses, not a random draw. Margins of error are given as a guide to precision, not as a guarantee that the results hold for every business in the country.
