    What this means for agencies

    what to fix first, and how to pitch it

    For small agencies and freelancers who sell websites to independent brokerages, agent teams and property managers. We scanned 133 of their websites: 2% score under 60 out of 100, and 86% leave at least three of the questions buyers, sellers and landlords ask unanswered. Here is where the winnable work is, which problems to lead a pitch with, and how to show them without days of spec design.

    5 problems to lead a pitch with

    1. 87%
      What will it cost me? Content

      Say plainly how the firm is paid: commission or flat fee and what it covers, or the management and leasing fees.

    2. 87%

      Raise text contrast to at least 4.5:1, especially text laid over hero photos.

      Parts of the site are hard to read, and it is a WCAG failure.
    3. 80%

      Show the broker of record and each agent’s licence number in the footer and on agent pages.

      A careful client checks the licence first, and your site makes them look it up elsewhere.
    4. 78%

    How to show a prospect their problems, and a redesign

    1. 1Lead with their problem, not your services

      Open with one question the site fails, for example "What will it cost me?" (87% of sites in this report miss it), and show the exact page where the answer should be.

    A1. The research question

    When someone wants to buy, sell or rent out a home and looks up an independent agent, brokerage or property manager online, usually on a phone, can they search what is for sale or rent, reach the business or ask for a showing, tell whether it works in their area, get a home valuation or rental analysis, see a track record, check a licence number, find out what it charges and see whether past clients vouch for it?

    Population. Independently owned US residential real estate businesses with their own website, in four groups: independent residential brokerages, solo agents and small teams, independent property managers, and luxury boutiques. These are the real estate businesses a small or mid-size web agency can realistically win as clients.

    A2. Who was studied

    The sample was built from local search listings, city by city, then screened so that every site in it is one an independent agency could realistically win.

    Included

    • An independently owned brokerage, team or property manager with its own website (not a directory, portal or social profile)
    • Based in the United States
    • Home page responds and shows the business it claims to be (each home page title was checked before scanning)

    Excluded

    • Franchise and national brokerages and their affiliates and agents (for example Keller Williams, RE/MAX, Coldwell Banker, Century 21, ERA, Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate, Compass, eXp, Real Brokerage, Berkshire Hathaway HomeServices, Sotheby's International Realty, Engel & Völkers, Howard Hanna, Douglas Elliman, Weichert, Redfin), whose agents' sites are templates

    • National and franchised property managers (for example Real Property Management, Mynd, Evernest, Greystar, Pathlight, Renters Warehouse, Mavis) and any business with offices in several states
    • Agent pages hosted on a franchise or portal domain
    • Parked, placeholder, password-protected or unreachable sites, and duplicates

    A3. How the sites were read

    Pages. The home page, then for each of nine topics (listings or rentals, home valuation, sold or track record, agents or team, about, reviews, areas or communities, fees or services, contact) the best-matching link on the home page.

    Speed and accessibility. Google PageSpeed Insights (Lighthouse) on a mid-range phone profile and on desktop, plus Chrome UX Report data from real visitors over the previous 28 days where Google publishes it for the site.

    Crawler conduct. One request at a time to each site, at least 400 ms apart, at most ten pages per site; robots.txt is read and obeyed for every page requested; a site that refuses is left out and never retried. The crawler identifies itself as Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; ClikitiResearch/1.0; +https://clikiti.com).

    A5. The website score

    Each site's overall score (0 to 100) is Clikiti's website score, rubric 2.0.0. It measures the website, not the business, and the client questions above do not feed it. It is a weighted mean of five pillars:

    PillarWeightBased on
    Usability30%Checks of the home page for broken links and images, and an expert review of the page as rendered on a phone and on a desktop screen, against Jakob Nielsen's ten usability heuristics. Each issue must name the heuristic, a severity on the Nielsen Norman Group scale and the exact element or wording; issues without all three are discarded, and two independent passes must agree.
    Speed25%Google Lighthouse on a mid-range phone (60%) and on desktop (20%), and real-visitor Core Web Vitals from the Chrome UX Report (20%) where available.
    Accessibility25%Google Lighthouse accessibility audits (70%) and Clikiti's own WCAG checks of the served page (30%).
    Search10%Google Lighthouse SEO audits (60%) and Clikiti's own search checks of the served page (40%).
    Trust10%Google Lighthouse best-practice audits (40%) and security header checks (60%).

    Within a pillar, each issue costs points by severity (critical, high, medium, low) and by how many times it occurs; issues based on judgement count at 70% of measured ones, and duplicates of the same root cause count once. On the four business profiles, the number out of 100 is this overall score, and each dot is one check in its group: filled when the site passes it, hollow when it does not.

    A6. Precision

    With 133 sites, a share reported for the whole sample is precise to within ±8.5 percentage points at 95% confidence (worst case, a 50% share; 1.96 × √(0.25/n)). By type: independent residential brokerages ±14.4 (n = 46); solo agents and small teams ±20.9 (n = 22); independent property managers ±14.3 (n = 47); luxury boutiques ±23.1 (n = 18). Differences between types smaller than these margins should be read as similar. Percentages are rounded to whole numbers; medians are the middle value of the sites in a group.

    A7. Quality control

    • Every home page in the cohort was fetched and its title checked against the listed business before scanning
    • Every home page was checked for gambling, pharmacy, adult and crypto spam (hijacked domains) before scanning
    • Detection rules were checked against the evidence they record for a sample of businesses in every group, and revised before the final run
    • Every published figure is recomputed from the data at build time, and a statement in the text that the data does not support stops the build
    • Results are published only after automated checks for missing data, anonymity, accessibility of the report itself and the margin of error statement pass

    A8. Limitations

    • A point-in-time snapshot: sites change, and a site may have improved or declined since it was read.
    • Only public pages were read: the home page and pages it links to. Listing-search tools, saved searches, chat widgets and multi-step valuation forms were not tested.
    • Answers are read from the page as a server delivers it. Content that only appears after scripts run in the browser (many listing searches, valuation tools and review widgets) is not seen unless the page links to it or loads it by name, which the study counts as a gap a search engine or a slow phone would also meet.
    • Licence-number display rules differ by state; the study reports whether a number is shown, not whether a business meets its state's advertising rules.
    • The study reports what a website shows. It does not judge any agent, brokerage, property manager, listing or price, or compliance with any licensing, advertising or fair housing rule.
    • Automated accessibility tests find only part of what WCAG covers; keyboard and screen-reader use need a manual audit.
    • The sample is a structured list of real, independent businesses, not a random draw. Margins of error are given as a guide to precision, not as a guarantee that the results hold for every business in the country.
