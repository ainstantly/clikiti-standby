# The State of Independent Med Spa Websites 2026 (Q4 2026)

**Answer:** Only 34% of the 194 independent med spa and aesthetic clinic websites we scanned show any treatment price: per unit, per syringe, per session or a starting price.

Clikiti scanned 194 independent med spa and aesthetic clinic websites in October 2026 and checked each one against 13 checks: 9 questions clients ask before choosing, plus accessibility and speed checks tied to WCAG 2.2 and Core Web Vitals.

## Key findings

- Only 34% of the 194 independent med spa and aesthetic clinic websites we scanned show any treatment price: per unit, per syringe, per session or a starting price.
- A typical site answers five of the nine questions clients ask before choosing (mean 5.1).
- The median overall score is 75 out of 100 (mean 75).
- The least-answered questions are "What will it cost?" (34%), "Is it safe, and how long will I look treated?" (42%) and "Is a doctor overseeing this?" (44%).
- The best-answered are "Can I book without calling?" (87%) and "Who will be doing my treatment?" (71%).
- Injector-led boutiques and aesthetic nurse practices have the highest median score (76), against 75 for independent med spas, 75 for laser and skin clinics and 74 for cosmetic dermatology.
- 87% fail "Text contrast passes 4.5:1" (WCAG 1.4.3).
- 77% fail "Loads in under 2.5 s on a phone" (Core Web Vitals · LCP).
- 59% fail "MedicalBusiness, MedicalClinic, Physician or DaySpa structured data" (Schema.org · MedicalBusiness).

### 5 fixable problems to lead a pitch with

1. **Text contrast passes 4.5:1** (87% of sites, quick fix). Fix: Raise text contrast to at least 4.5:1 in the theme: pale taupe on cream and white on photographs are the usual failures. Pitch: "The soft palette makes parts of the site hard to read, and it is a WCAG failure."
2. **Loads in under 2.5 s on a phone** (77% of sites, build). Fix: Resize and compress the hero video or image and the gallery, defer booking and chat scripts, aim for under 2.5 s on a phone. Pitch: "Your home page takes several seconds to appear on the phone where clients compare you with two other practices."
3. **What will it cost?** (66% of sites, content). Fix: Publish the price the practice already quotes on the phone: neurotoxin per unit, filler per syringe, laser and facials per session or package, or a "from" price for each treatment. Pitch: "The first thing a client wants to know is what it costs, and your site makes her book a consultation to find out."
4. **MedicalBusiness, MedicalClinic, Physician or DaySpa structured data** (59% of sites, quick fix). Fix: Add MedicalBusiness (or MedicalClinic or DaySpa) structured data with the address, phone, hours and the physician. Pitch: "Search engines and assistants have to guess what kind of practice you are and where you are."
5. **Is it safe, and how long will I look treated?** (58% of sites, content). Fix: On each treatment page, say how long the treatment takes, the usual downtime and the common side effects, and link the aftercare instructions. Pitch: "Clients plan treatments around work and events. Your site never says how long they will look treated."

### How to show a prospect their problems and a redesign

1. **Lead with their problem, not your services.** Open with one question the site fails, for example text contrast passes 4.5:1 (87% of sites in this report miss it), and show the exact page where the answer should be.
2. **Put a number on it.** Compare their site with the sector: across 194 independent med spas, cosmetic dermatology practices, laser clinics and injector-led boutiques sites, the median scores 75 out of 100 and answers 5 of the 9 questions clients ask. A number travels further inside the client than an opinion.
3. **Show the fix without days of spec work.** A rough redesign that answers those questions persuades better than a proposal, but it usually costs a designer a day or two per prospect. You can sketch it by hand, or use Clikiti: the scan is free and shows any site's problems in about a minute, and paid plans draft a redesign of the home page from the prospect's own brand, from about $2.33 per pitch.

## Method

- **Sample:** 194 independent med spa and aesthetic clinic websites (66 independent med spas, 46 cosmetic dermatology, 39 laser and skin clinics, 43 injector-led boutiques and aesthetic nurse practices).
- **When:** scanned October 2026; published 2026-10-15.
- **How:** the questions are read from the served HTML of each home page and the pages it links to. Load time, contrast and image descriptions come from Google Lighthouse (PageSpeed Insights) in a phone and a desktop render, with Chrome UX Report real-user data where it exists. A check passes only when the scan finds it; each check cites the standard it comes from.
- **Score:** Clikiti's overall website score (rubric v2): usability 30%, speed 25%, accessibility 25%, SEO 10%, trust 10%. It measures the website, not the independent med spa and aesthetic clinic. The questions are reported separately and do not feed it.
- **Margin of error:** shares are exact for the sites scanned. Read as estimates for all independent med spa and aesthetic clinic websites, they carry a margin of error of up to ±7 percentage points at 95% confidence (worst case, p = 0.5, n = 194).
- **Privacy:** results are published as aggregates or anonymously. Sites are listed by name in the appendix with no individual results; only the best-in-class example is named with its results.

## Appendix A: Methodology

194 websites, 4 types of organisation, 47 states, read on 2026-10-15. Margin of error ±7 points at 95% confidence.

### A1. The research question

When someone considering an aesthetic treatment (injectables, laser, skin) looks up an independent med spa or clinic online, usually on a phone, can they find out what it costs, whether they can book without calling, who will treat them and with what credential, whether a doctor oversees the practice, what results look like, whether they can spread the cost, whether they can talk to someone first, what the downtime and risks are, and whether other clients vouch for it?

Population: Independently owned US aesthetics businesses with one to three locations and their own website, in four groups: independent med spas, cosmetic dermatology practices, laser and skin clinics, and injector-led boutiques and aesthetic nurse practices. These are the aesthetics businesses a small or mid-size web agency can realistically win as clients.

### A2. Who was studied

| Source | What it contributes | Sites |
|---|---|---:|
| Local search, med spas | Independent medical spas offering injectables, lasers and skin treatments, found in local search results, city by city | 87 |
| Local search, cosmetic dermatology | Independent dermatology practices with a cosmetic or aesthetic service line, found in local search results, city by city | 49 |
| Local search, laser and skin clinics | Independent laser hair removal, laser skin and skin clinics found in local search results, city by city | 114 |
| Local search, injectors | Injector-led boutiques and aesthetic nurse practices (Botox and filler studios run by a nurse injector, nurse practitioner or physician assistant), found in local search results, city by city | 118 |

Included:
- An independently owned business with one to three locations and its own website (not a directory, booking-platform page or social profile)
- Based in the United States
- Offers medical aesthetic treatments (injectables, lasers or energy devices, medical-grade skin treatments)
- Home page responds and shows the business it claims to be (each home page title was checked before scanning)

Excluded:
- National and regional chains and franchises (for example Ideal Image, LaserAway, Sono Bello, SkinSpirit, Ever/Body, Massage Envy, Hand & Stone, European Wax Center, Woodhouse, Radiance, Skin Laundry, The Lux) and any business with four or more locations or in several states
- Hospital systems, university clinics and dermatology groups owned by a private-equity platform with many locations
- Plastic surgery practices whose site is about surgery first
- Day spas and salons that offer no medical aesthetic treatment
- Directories, marketplaces and booking-platform-only pages (RealSelf, Yelp, Vagaro, Boulevard or GlossGenius pages without the business's own site)
- Parked, placeholder, password-protected, hijacked or unreachable sites, and duplicates

### A3. How the sites were read

- Pages: The home page, then for each of nine topics (treatments, prices, team, before and after, financing and memberships, reviews, booking, FAQ, contact) the best-matching link on the home page.
- Speed and accessibility: Google PageSpeed Insights (Lighthouse) on a mid-range phone profile and on desktop, plus Chrome UX Report data from real visitors over the previous 28 days where Google publishes it for the site.
- Crawler conduct: One request at a time to each site, at least 400 ms apart, at most ten pages per site; robots.txt is read and obeyed for every page requested; a site that refuses is left out and never retried. User agent: `Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; ClikitiResearch/1.0; +https://clikiti.com)`.

### A5. The website score

Clikiti website score, rubric 2.0.0: a weighted mean of usability 30%, speed 25%, accessibility 25%, search 10% and trust 10%. It measures the website, not the organisation; the questions above do not feed it. Report-card letters: A 90+, B 75+, C 45+, D 30+, F below 30.

### A6. Precision

±7 points overall at 95% confidence (worst case p = 0.5, 1.96 × √(0.25/n)); by type: independent med spas ±12.1 (n = 66); cosmetic dermatology ±14.4 (n = 46); laser and skin clinics ±15.7 (n = 39); injector-led boutiques and aesthetic nurse practices ±14.9 (n = 43).

### A7. Quality control

- Every home page in the cohort was fetched and its title checked against the listed business before scanning
- Every home page was checked for signs of a hijacked domain (gambling, adult, crypto or unrelated pharmacy content) before scanning
- Detection rules were checked against the evidence they record for a sample of businesses in every group, and revised before the final run
- Every published figure is recomputed from the data at build time, and a statement in the text that the data does not support stops the build
- Results are published only after automated checks for missing data, anonymity, accessibility of the report itself and the margin of error statement pass

### A8. Limitations

- A point-in-time snapshot: sites change, and a site may have improved or declined since it was read.
- Only public pages were read: the home page and pages it links to. Booking flows, chat widgets and quiz-style consultation forms were not tested.
- Answers are read from the page as a server delivers it. Content that only appears after scripts run in the browser (some price menus, schedulers, galleries and review widgets) is not seen, which the study counts as a gap a search engine or a slow phone would also meet.
- 21 candidates blocked or did not answer automated visitors, or served a placeholder, and are not in the study; sites behind such protection may differ from those studied.
- The study reports what a website shows. It does not judge any treatment, result, safety record or price, and it does not assess compliance with any state's rules on medical supervision, delegation, advertising or before-and-after photos.
- Automated accessibility tests find only part of what WCAG covers; keyboard and screen-reader use need a manual audit.
- The sample is a structured list of real, independent businesses, not a random draw. Margins of error are given as a guide to precision, not as a guarantee that the results hold for every business in the country.
