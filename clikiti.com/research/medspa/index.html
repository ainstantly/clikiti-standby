Summary of findings

Clients ask nine questions. The median site answers four.

      05 · Method in brief

      How the sites were read

      Step 1 · all 194 sites

      Read

      The home page and up to nine pages it links to (treatments, prices, team, before and after, financing and memberships, reviews, booking, FAQ, contact), read as a server delivers them. The client questions are answered here, by fixed published rules (Appendix A4).

      Step 2 · all 194 sites

      Measure

      Google Lighthouse (PageSpeed Insights) in a phone and a desktop render, with Chrome UX Report field data where it exists: load time, contrast and image descriptions. Repeatable.

      Step 3 · all 194 sites, score only

      Review

      An expert usability review of each home page against Nielsen's heuristics, used only in the usability part of the score. Every issue must name the heuristic, a severity and the element, or it is dropped.

      ScopeThis study examines how independent med spas and aesthetic clinics present themselves on the web, not the safety, results or prices of their treatments, nor whether they meet any state's rules on medical supervision.

      What this means for agencies

      For small agencies and freelancers who sell websites to independent med spas and aesthetic clinics. We scanned 194 of their websites: 2% score under 60 out of 100, and 73% leave at least three of the questions clients ask unanswered. Here is where the winnable work is, which problems to lead a pitch with, and how to show them without days of spec design.

      5 problems to lead a pitch with

      1. 87%
        Text contrast passes 4.5:1 Quick fix

        Raise text contrast to at least 4.5:1 in the theme: pale taupe on cream and white on photographs are the usual failures.

        The soft palette makes parts of the site hard to read, and it is a WCAG failure.
      2. 77%
        Loads in under 2.5 s on a phone Build

        Resize and compress the hero video or image and the gallery, defer booking and chat scripts, aim for under 2.5 s on a phone.

        Your home page takes several seconds to appear on the phone where clients compare you with two other practices.
      3. 66%
        What will it cost? Content

        Publish the price the practice already quotes on the phone: neurotoxin per unit, filler per syringe, laser and facials per session or package, or a "from" price for each treatment.

        The first thing a client wants to know is what it costs, and your site makes her book a consultation to find out.
      4. 59%
        MedicalBusiness, MedicalClinic, Physician or DaySpa structured data Quick fix

        Add MedicalBusiness (or MedicalClinic or DaySpa) structured data with the address, phone, hours and the physician.

        Search engines and assistants have to guess what kind of practice you are and where you are.
      5. 58%
        Is it safe, and how long will I look treated? Content

        On each treatment page, say how long the treatment takes, the usual downtime and the common side effects, and link the aftercare instructions.

        Clients plan treatments around work and events. Your site never says how long they will look treated.

      A1. The research question

      When someone considering an aesthetic treatment (injectables, laser, skin) looks up an independent med spa or clinic online, usually on a phone, can they find out what it costs, whether they can book without calling, who will treat them and with what credential, whether a doctor oversees the practice, what results look like, whether they can spread the cost, whether they can talk to someone first, what the downtime and risks are, and whether other clients vouch for it?

      Population. Independently owned US aesthetics businesses with one to three locations and their own website, in four groups: independent med spas, cosmetic dermatology practices, laser and skin clinics, and injector-led boutiques and aesthetic nurse practices. These are the aesthetics businesses a small or mid-size web agency can realistically win as clients.

      A2. Who was studied

      The sample was built from local search listings, city by city, then screened so that every site in it is one an independent agency could realistically win.

      Included

      • An independently owned business with one to three locations and its own website (not a directory, booking-platform page or social profile)
      • Based in the United States
      • Offers medical aesthetic treatments (injectables, lasers or energy devices, medical-grade skin treatments)
      • Home page responds and shows the business it claims to be (each home page title was checked before scanning)

      Excluded

      • National and regional chains and franchises (for example Ideal Image, LaserAway, Sono Bello, SkinSpirit, Ever/Body, Massage Envy, Hand & Stone, European Wax Center, Woodhouse, Radiance, Skin Laundry, The Lux) and any business with four or more locations or in several states

      • Plastic surgery practices whose site is about surgery first
      • Day spas and salons that offer no medical aesthetic treatment
      • Directories, marketplaces and booking-platform-only pages (RealSelf, Yelp, Vagaro, Boulevard or GlossGenius pages without the business's own site)
      • Parked, placeholder, password-protected, hijacked or unreachable sites, and duplicates

      A3. How the sites were read

      Pages. The home page, then for each of nine topics (treatments, prices, team, before and after, financing and memberships, reviews, booking, FAQ, contact) the best-matching link on the home page.

      Speed and accessibility. Google PageSpeed Insights (Lighthouse) on a mid-range phone profile and on desktop, plus Chrome UX Report data from real visitors over the previous 28 days where Google publishes it for the site.

      Crawler conduct. One request at a time to each site, at least 400 ms apart, at most ten pages per site; robots.txt is read and obeyed for every page requested; a site that refuses is left out and never retried. The crawler identifies itself as Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; ClikitiResearch/1.0; +https://clikiti.com).

      A5. The website score

      Each site's overall score (0 to 100) is Clikiti's website score, rubric 2.0.0. It measures the website, not the practice, and the client questions above do not feed it. It is a weighted mean of five pillars:

      PillarWeightBased on
      Usability30%Checks of the home page for broken links and images, and an expert review of the page as rendered on a phone and on a desktop screen, against Jakob Nielsen's ten usability heuristics. Each issue must name the heuristic, a severity on the Nielsen Norman Group scale and the exact element or wording; issues without all three are discarded, and two independent passes must agree.
      Speed25%Google Lighthouse on a mid-range phone (60%) and on desktop (20%), and real-visitor Core Web Vitals from the Chrome UX Report (20%) where available.
      Accessibility25%Google Lighthouse accessibility audits (70%) and Clikiti's own WCAG checks of the served page (30%).
      Search10%Google Lighthouse SEO audits (60%) and Clikiti's own search checks of the served page (40%).
      Trust10%Google Lighthouse best-practice audits (40%) and security header checks (60%).

      Within a pillar, each issue costs points by severity (critical, high, medium, low) and by how many times it occurs; issues based on judgement count at 70% of measured ones, and duplicates of the same root cause count once. On the four practice profiles, the number out of 100 is this overall score, and each dot is one check in its group: filled when the site passes it, hollow when it does not.

      A6. Precision

      With 194 sites, a share reported for the whole sample is precise to within ±7 percentage points at 95% confidence (worst case, a 50% share; 1.96 × √(0.25/n)). By type: independent med spas ±12.1 (n = 66); cosmetic dermatology ±14.4 (n = 46); laser and skin clinics ±15.7 (n = 39); injector-led boutiques and aesthetic nurse practices ±14.9 (n = 43). Differences between types smaller than these margins should be read as similar. Percentages are rounded to whole numbers; medians are the middle value of the sites in a group.

      A7. Quality control

      • Every home page in the cohort was fetched and its title checked against the listed business before scanning
      • Every home page was checked for signs of a hijacked domain (gambling, adult, crypto or unrelated pharmacy content) before scanning
      • Detection rules were checked against the evidence they record for a sample of businesses in every group, and revised before the final run
      • Every published figure is recomputed from the data at build time, and a statement in the text that the data does not support stops the build
      • Results are published only after automated checks for missing data, anonymity, accessibility of the report itself and the margin of error statement pass

      A8. Limitations

      • A point-in-time snapshot: sites change, and a site may have improved or declined since it was read.
      • Only public pages were read: the home page and pages it links to. Booking flows, chat widgets and quiz-style consultation forms were not tested.
      • Answers are read from the page as a server delivers it. Content that only appears after scripts run in the browser (some price menus, schedulers, galleries and review widgets) is not seen, which the study counts as a gap a search engine or a slow phone would also meet.
      • 21 candidates blocked or did not answer automated visitors, or served a placeholder, and are not in the study; sites behind such protection may differ from those studied.
      • The study reports what a website shows. It does not judge any treatment, result, safety record or price, and it does not assess compliance with any state's rules on medical supervision, delegation, advertising or before-and-after photos.
      • Automated accessibility tests find only part of what WCAG covers; keyboard and screen-reader use need a manual audit.
      • The sample is a structured list of real, independent businesses, not a random draw. Margins of error are given as a guide to precision, not as a guarantee that the results hold for every business in the country.
