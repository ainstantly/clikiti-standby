# The State of Independent Wedding Venue Websites 2026 (Q4 2026)

**Answer:** Only 9% of the 191 independent wedding venue websites we scanned show which dates are still open.

*Free to use and cite under CC BY 4.0. Credit Clikiti with a link.* For small agencies and freelancers who build websites for independent wedding and event venues. Clikiti scanned 191 independent wedding venue websites in October 2026 and checked each one against 13 checks: 9 questions couples ask before choosing, plus accessibility and speed checks tied to WCAG 2.2 and Core Web Vitals.

## Key findings

- Only 9% of the 191 independent wedding venue websites we scanned show which dates are still open.
- A typical site answers five of the nine questions couples ask before choosing (mean 4.8).
- The median overall score is 77 out of 100 (mean 76).
- The least-answered questions are "Is our date still free?" (9%), "What if it rains?" (36%) and "Do other couples vouch for you?" (41%).
- The best-answered are "Can we see real weddings here?" (95%) and "How many guests can you hold?" (76%).
- By kind of business, the highest median score is 78 (lofts and restaurants with event space), against 77 for barns and farms, 77 for vineyards and wineries and 76 for estates and historic houses.
- 76% fail "Text contrast passes 4.5:1" (WCAG 1.4.3).
- 72% fail "Loads in under 2.5 s on a phone" (Core Web Vitals · LCP).
- 43% fail "Event venue or local business structured data" (Schema.org · EventVenue).

### 5 fixable problems to lead a pitch with

1. **Is our date still free?** (91% of sites, build). Fix: An availability page linked from the home page: open dates listed by month, or a calendar the venue updates when a date books. Pitch: ""Is our date free?" is the first question in every inquiry. Answer it on the site and the inquiries you get are for dates you have."
2. **Text contrast passes 4.5:1** (76% of sites, quick fix). Fix: Raise text contrast to at least 4.5:1, especially light script and thin text over photographs. Pitch: "Pale text on a pale photo is hard to read for the parents and grandparents who look too, and it is a WCAG failure."
3. **Loads in under 2.5 s on a phone** (72% of sites, build). Fix: Resize and compress the hero photographs and slideshow, lazy-load the gallery and defer scripts; aim for under 2.5 s on a phone. Pitch: "Your home page takes several seconds to appear on a phone, which is where couples look first."
4. **What if it rains?** (64% of sites, quick fix). Fix: Name the rain plan: where the ceremony moves, when the call is made and whether a tent or covered space is included in the fee. Pitch: "Every couple booking an outdoor ceremony asks about rain. A named plan on the page answers it before the tour."
5. **Do other couples vouch for you?** (59% of sites, quick fix). Fix: Quote couples on the home and weddings pages, or embed the venue’s reviews, with Review or AggregateRating markup. Pitch: "Couples praise you on The Knot and Google, and your own site keeps it quiet."

### How to show a prospect their problems and a redesign

1. **Lead with their problem, not your services.** Open with one question the site fails, for example "Is our date still free?" (91% of sites in this report miss it), and show the exact page where the answer should be.
2. **Put a number on it.** Compare their site with the sector: across 191 independent barn, estate, vineyard and loft wedding venues sites, the median scores 77 out of 100 and answers 5 of the 9 questions couples ask. A number travels further inside the client than an opinion.
3. **Show the fix without days of spec work.** A rough redesign that answers those questions persuades better than a proposal, but it usually costs a designer a day or two per prospect. You can sketch it by hand, or use Clikiti: the scan is free and shows any site's problems in about a minute, and paid plans draft a redesign of the home page from the prospect's own brand, from about $2.33 per pitch.

## Method

- **Sample:** 191 independent wedding venue websites (62 barns and farms, 45 estates and historic houses, 45 vineyards and wineries, 39 lofts and restaurants with event space).
- **When:** scanned October 2026; published 2026-10-16.
- **How:** the questions are read from the served HTML of each home page and the pages it links to. Load time, contrast and image descriptions come from Google Lighthouse (PageSpeed Insights) in a phone and a desktop render, with Chrome UX Report real-user data where it exists. A check passes only when the scan finds it; each check cites the standard it comes from.
- **Score:** Clikiti's overall website score (rubric v2): usability 30%, speed 25%, accessibility 25%, SEO 10%, trust 10%. It measures the website, not the independent wedding venue. The questions are reported separately and do not feed it.
- **Margin of error:** shares are exact for the sites scanned. Read as estimates for all independent wedding venue websites, they carry a margin of error of up to ±7.1 percentage points at 95% confidence (worst case, p = 0.5, n = 191).
- **Privacy:** results are published as aggregates or anonymously. Sites are listed by name in the appendix with no individual results; only the best-in-class example is named with its results.

## Appendix A: Methodology

191 websites, 4 types of organisation, 30 states, read on 2026-10-15 to 2026-10-16. Margin of error ±7.1 points at 95% confidence.

### A1. The research question

When a couple planning a wedding looks an independent venue up online, usually on a phone, can they find out what it costs, how many guests it holds, whether their date is free, what the fee includes, whether they can bring their own caterer, what happens if it rains, whether they can book a tour online, what real weddings there looked like and whether other couples vouch for it?

Population: Independently owned US wedding and event venues with one to three properties and their own website, in four groups: barns and farms, estates and historic houses, vineyards and wineries, and city lofts and restaurants with an event space. These are the venues a small or mid-size web agency can realistically win as clients.

### A2. Who was studied

| Source | What it contributes | Sites |
|---|---|---:|
| Local search, barns and farms | Barn, farm and ranch wedding venues found in local search results, city by city | 62 |
| Local search, estates and historic houses | Privately run estates, manors, mansions and historic houses that host weddings, found in local search results, city by city | 52 |
| Local search, vineyards and wineries | Independent wineries and vineyards with a weddings page, found in local search results, city by city | 46 |
| Local search, lofts and restaurants | Independent city lofts, industrial event spaces and restaurants or breweries with a private event space for weddings, found in local search results, city by city | 35 |

Included:
- An independently owned venue with one to three properties and its own website (not a directory, marketplace or social profile)
- Hosts weddings and says so on its site
- Based in the United States
- Home page responds and shows the venue it claims to be (each home page title was checked before scanning)

Excluded:
- Hotel chains and resort groups, and hotel ballrooms
- Venue groups and hospitality groups running four or more venues (for example Wedgewood Weddings), and wineries owned by large wine corporations
- Marketplace and listing pages (The Knot, WeddingWire, Zola, Here Comes the Guide, Peerspace, Yelp) and social profiles
- Members-only country clubs with no public wedding page
- Municipal, county and state-park venues, and house museums run by a government body
- Directories, lead-generation sites, franchises and national brands
- Parked, placeholder, password-protected, hijacked or unreachable sites, and duplicates

### A3. How the sites were read

- Pages: The home page, then for each of twelve topics (weddings, events, pricing and packages, the spaces, gallery, FAQ, vendors and catering, tours, availability, reviews, about, contact) the best-matching link on the home page, and up to two pricing, FAQ or tour links on the weddings page.
- Speed and accessibility: Google PageSpeed Insights (Lighthouse) on a mid-range phone profile and on desktop, plus Chrome UX Report data from real visitors over the previous 28 days where Google publishes it for the site.
- Crawler conduct: One request at a time to each site, at least 400 ms apart, at most thirteen pages per site; robots.txt is read and obeyed for every page requested; a site that refuses is left out and never retried. User agent: `Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; ClikitiResearch/1.0; +https://clikiti.com)`.

### A5. The website score

Clikiti website score, rubric 2.0.0: a weighted mean of usability 30%, speed 25%, accessibility 25%, search 10% and trust 10%. It measures the website, not the organisation; the questions above do not feed it. Report-card letters: A 90+, B 75+, C 45+, D 30+, F below 30.

### A6. Precision

±7.1 points overall at 95% confidence (worst case p = 0.5, 1.96 × √(0.25/n)); by type: barns and farms ±12.4 (n = 62); estates and historic houses ±14.6 (n = 45); vineyards and wineries ±14.6 (n = 45); lofts and restaurants with event space ±15.7 (n = 39).

### A7. Quality control

- Every home page in the cohort was fetched and its title checked against the listed venue before scanning
- Every home page was checked for hijacked content (gambling, pharmacy, adult and crypto spam) before scanning
- Every home page was checked for a second website of the same venue (shared phone numbers and names)
- Detection rules were checked against the evidence they record for a sample of venues in every group, and revised before the final run
- Every published figure is recomputed from the data at build time, and a statement in the text that the data does not support stops the build
- Results are published only after automated checks for missing data, anonymity, accessibility of the report itself and the margin of error statement pass

### A8. Limitations

- A point-in-time snapshot: sites change, and a venue's prices and open dates change through the year.
- Only public pages were read: the home page and pages it links to. Downloadable brochures and PDF price sheets, inquiry flows behind a form and client portals were not read, so a venue that sends its prices only on request fails the price question.
- Answers are read from the page as a server delivers it. Content that only appears after scripts run in the browser (some calendars, schedulers and review widgets) is not seen, which the study counts as a gap a search engine or a slow phone would also meet.
- Candidates that blocked automated visitors are not in the study; sites behind such protection may differ from those studied.
- The study reports what a website shows. It does not judge any venue's property, catering, service or prices.
- Automated accessibility tests find only part of what WCAG covers; keyboard and screen-reader use need a manual audit.
- The sample is a structured list of real, independent venues, not a random draw. Margins of error are given as a guide to precision, not as a guarantee that the results hold for every venue in the country.
