        05 · Method in brief

        How the sites were read

        Step 1 · all 185 sites

        Read

        The home page and up to eleven pages it links to (services, quote, prices, packing, storage, long distance, insurance or valuation, FAQ, policies, reviews, contact), read as a server delivers them. The customer questions are answered here, by fixed published rules (Appendix A4).

        Step 2 · all 185 sites

        Measure

        Google Lighthouse (PageSpeed Insights) in a phone and a desktop render, with Chrome UX Report field data where it exists: load time, contrast and image descriptions. Repeatable.

        Step 3 · all 185 sites, score only

        Review

        An expert usability review of each home page against Nielsen's heuristics, used only in the usability part of the score. Every issue must name the heuristic, a severity and the element, or it is dropped.

        ScopeThis study examines how independent moving companies present themselves on the web, not the quality of their moves, crews, care of belongings or prices, and it does not check any USDOT or MC number against the federal register.

        What this means for agencies

        what to fix first, and how to pitch it

        For small agencies and freelancers who sell websites to independent moving companies. We scanned 185 of their websites: 1% score under 60 out of 100, and 82% leave at least three of the questions customers planning a move ask unanswered. Here is where the winnable work is, which problems to lead a pitch with, and how to show them without days of spec design.

        Who is realistic to win

        Long-distance and interstate movers are the most winnable: the lowest median score in this report (75 out of 100), and 0% of them score under 60. Their problems are visible, specific and fixable by a small team.

        5 problems to lead a pitch with

        1. 87%
          Loads in under 2.5 s on a phone Build

          Resize and compress the hero image, defer chat and tracking scripts, aim for under 2.5 s on a phone.

          Your home page takes several seconds to appear on a customer's phone.
        2. 84%
          Text contrast passes 4.5:1 Quick fix

          Raise text contrast to at least 4.5:1 in the theme, especially white text over truck and crew photos.

          Parts of the site are hard to read, and it is a WCAG failure.
        3. 81%
          Is the price fixed? Content

          One paragraph, linked from the quote form, that says which kind of estimate the company gives (binding, non-binding or not-to-exceed) and what can change the price on the day.

          "Is the price fixed?" is the question behind every bad mover story, and your site never answers it.
        4. 76%
          What's the deposit, and can I cancel? Quick fix

          State the deposit (amount or "no deposit") and the cancellation and rescheduling terms, near the quote form and in the FAQ.

          A customer about to commit wants to know what they lose if the closing date moves, and your site does not say.
        5. 69%
          What if something breaks? Content

          A short page on what happens if something breaks: released value (60 cents per pound per article) versus full value protection, what each costs and how to claim.

          Customers find out what basic coverage pays only after something breaks; the movers who explain it first get fewer one-star reviews.

        Share of sites with the problem. WCAG items are accessibility failures. Every check cites a standard in the method section.

        1. 1Lead with their problem, not your services

        A1. The research question

        When someone planning a move looks up an independent moving company online, usually on a phone, can they get a quote without calling, find out whether the price is fixed, what an hour or a move costs, whether the company is licensed to move them, what happens if something breaks, whether it will pack for them, whether it is free on their date, what the deposit and cancellation terms are, and whether other customers vouch for it?

        Population. Independently owned US moving companies with one to three locations, their own trucks and crews and their own website, in four groups: local residential movers, long-distance and interstate movers, moving and storage companies, and specialty movers (piano, senior and office). These are the moving businesses a small or mid-size web agency can realistically win as clients.

        A2. Who was studied

        The sample was built from local search listings, city by city, then screened so that every site in it is one an independent agency could realistically win.

        Local search, long-distance and interstate moversIndependent long-distance and interstate moving companies found in local search results, city by city28
        Local search, moving and storageIndependent moving companies that run their own storage warehouse, found in local search results, city by city31
        Local search, specialty moversIndependent piano movers, senior move managers with crews, and office and commercial movers found in local search results, city by city67

        Included

        • An independently owned business with one to three locations and its own website (not a directory, marketplace or social profile)
        • Moves people's belongings with its own crews and trucks: not a broker, a lead service, a truck or container rental or a labour-only marketplace
        • Based in the United States
        • Home page responds and shows the business it claims to be (each home page title was checked before scanning)

        Excluded

        • Van lines and their agents' national brand pages (for example Allied, North American, Atlas, Mayflower, United, Bekins, Wheaton) and franchises (for example Two Men and a Truck, College Hunks Hauling Junk and Moving, All My Sons, Gentle Giant, Mighty Movers), and any business with four or more locations
        • Truck and container rental companies (for example U-Haul, PODS, U-Pack) and labour-only marketplaces
        • Moving brokers and lead-generation sites: any site that sells leads, books other carriers or says it connects customers with movers
        • Directories and review sites (for example Yelp, Angi, Thumbtack, moving.com, Moving Authority) and generic keyword domains that hide the business
        • Parked, placeholder, under-maintenance, password-protected or unreachable sites, hijacked domains and duplicates

        A3. How the sites were read

        Pages. The home page, then for each of eleven topics (services, quote or estimate, prices or rates, packing, storage, long distance, insurance or valuation, FAQ, policies or terms, reviews, contact) the best-matching link on the home page.

        Speed and accessibility. Google PageSpeed Insights (Lighthouse) on a mid-range phone profile and on desktop, plus Chrome UX Report data from real visitors over the previous 28 days where Google publishes it for the site.

        Crawler conduct. One request at a time to each site, at least 400 ms apart, at most twelve pages per site; robots.txt is read and obeyed for every page requested; a site that refuses is left out and never retried. The crawler identifies itself as Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; ClikitiResearch/1.0; +https://clikiti.com).

        A5. The website score

        Each site's overall score (0 to 100) is Clikiti's website score, rubric 2.0.0. It measures the website, not the mover, and the customer questions above do not feed it. It is a weighted mean of five pillars:

        PillarWeightBased on
        Usability30%Checks of the home page for broken links and images, and an expert review of the page as rendered on a phone and on a desktop screen, against Jakob Nielsen's ten usability heuristics. Each issue must name the heuristic, a severity on the Nielsen Norman Group scale and the exact element or wording; issues without all three are discarded, and two independent passes must agree.
        Speed25%Google Lighthouse on a mid-range phone (60%) and on desktop (20%), and real-visitor Core Web Vitals from the Chrome UX Report (20%) where available.
        Accessibility25%Google Lighthouse accessibility audits (70%) and Clikiti's own WCAG checks of the served page (30%).
        Search10%Google Lighthouse SEO audits (60%) and Clikiti's own search checks of the served page (40%).
        Trust10%Google Lighthouse best-practice audits (40%) and security header checks (60%).

        Within a pillar, each issue costs points by severity (critical, high, medium, low) and by how many times it occurs; issues based on judgement count at 70% of measured ones, and duplicates of the same root cause count once. On the four mover profiles, the number out of 100 is this overall score, and each dot is one check in its group: filled when the site passes it, hollow when it does not.

        A6. Precision

        With 185 sites, a share reported for the whole sample is precise to within ±7.2 percentage points at 95% confidence (worst case, a 50% share; 1.96 × √(0.25/n)). By type: local residential movers ±11.4 (n = 74); long-distance and interstate movers ±16.3 (n = 36); moving and storage companies ±15.3 (n = 41); specialty movers: piano, senior and office ±16.8 (n = 34). Differences between types smaller than these margins should be read as similar. Percentages are rounded to whole numbers; medians are the middle value of the sites in a group.

        A7. Quality control

        • Every home page in the cohort was fetched and its title checked against the listed business before scanning, and every home page was checked for gambling, pharmacy, adult and crypto spam
        • Businesses were checked for duplicates across domains by phone number
        • Detection rules were checked against the evidence they record for a sample of passes and fails for every rule, and revised before the final run
        • Every published figure is recomputed from the data at build time, and a statement in the text that the data does not support stops the build
        • Results are published only after automated checks for missing data, anonymity, accessibility of the report itself and the margin of error statement pass

        A8. Limitations

        • A point-in-time snapshot: sites change, and a site may have improved or declined since it was read. Moving is seasonal, and the scan ran in October, after the summer peak, when some companies run off-season offers.
        • Only public pages were read: the home page and pages it links to. Chat widgets, multi-step quote forms, inventory tools and customer portals were not tested.
        • Answers are read from the page as a server delivers it. Content that only appears after scripts run in the browser (some whole sites, quote calculators and review widgets) is not seen, which the study counts as a gap a search engine or a slow phone would also meet.
        • The study reports what a website shows. It does not judge any company's moves, crews or prices, and it does not check any USDOT or MC number, licence, insurance or valuation offer against a register or a policy.
        • Automated accessibility tests find only part of what WCAG covers; keyboard and screen-reader use need a manual audit.
        • The sample is a structured list of real, independent businesses, not a random draw. Margins of error are given as a guide to precision, not as a guarantee that the results hold for every business in the country.
