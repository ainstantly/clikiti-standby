**Answer:** Only 19% of the 185 independent moving company websites we scanned say whether their estimate is binding, non-binding or not-to-exceed.

Clikiti scanned 185 independent moving company websites in October 2026 and checked each one against 13 checks: 9 questions customers planning a move ask before choosing, plus accessibility and speed checks tied to WCAG 2.2 and Core Web Vitals.

## Key findings

- Only 19% of the 185 independent moving company websites we scanned say whether their estimate is binding, non-binding or not-to-exceed.
- A typical site answers four of the nine questions customers planning a move ask before choosing (mean 4.1).
- The median overall score is 76 out of 100 (mean 77).
- The least-answered questions are "Is the price fixed?" (19%), "What's the deposit, and can I cancel?" (24%) and "What if something breaks?" (31%).
- The best-answered are "Will you pack for me?" (85%) and "Can I get a quote without calling?" (84%).
- By kind of business, the highest median score is 79 (local residential movers), against 78 for specialty movers: piano, senior and office, 76 for moving and storage companies and 75 for long-distance and interstate movers.
- 87% fail "Loads in under 2.5 s on a phone" (Core Web Vitals · LCP).
- 84% fail "Text contrast passes 4.5:1" (WCAG 1.4.3).
- 36% fail "MovingCompany or local business structured data" (Schema.org · MovingCompany).

## What this means for agencies

For small agencies and freelancers who sell websites to independent moving companies. ### 5 fixable problems to lead a pitch with

1. **Loads in under 2.5 s on a phone** (87% of sites, build). Fix: Resize and compress the hero image, defer chat and tracking scripts, aim for under 2.5 s on a phone. Pitch: "Your home page takes several seconds to appear on a customer's phone."
2. **Text contrast passes 4.5:1** (84% of sites, quick fix). Fix: Raise text contrast to at least 4.5:1 in the theme, especially white text over truck and crew photos. Pitch: "Parts of the site are hard to read, and it is a WCAG failure."
3. **Is the price fixed?** (81% of sites, content). Fix: One paragraph, linked from the quote form, that says which kind of estimate the company gives (binding, non-binding or not-to-exceed) and what can change the price on the day. Pitch: ""Is the price fixed?" is the question behind every bad mover story, and your site never answers it."
4. **What's the deposit, and can I cancel?** (76% of sites, quick fix). Fix: State the deposit (amount or "no deposit") and the cancellation and rescheduling terms, near the quote form and in the FAQ. Pitch: "A customer about to commit wants to know what they lose if the closing date moves, and your site does not say."
5. **What if something breaks?** (69% of sites, content). Fix: A short page on what happens if something breaks: released value (60 cents per pound per article) versus full value protection, what each costs and how to claim. Pitch: "Customers find out what basic coverage pays only after something breaks; the movers who explain it first get fewer one-star reviews."

### How to show a prospect their problems and a redesign

1. **Lead with their problem, not your services.** Open with one question the site fails, for example loads in under 2.5 s on a phone (87% of sites in this report miss it), and show the exact page where the answer should be.
2. **Put a number on it.** Compare their site with the sector: across 185 independent local, long-distance, storage and specialty moving companies sites, the median scores 76 out of 100 and answers 4 of the 9 questions customers planning a move ask. A number travels further inside the client than an opinion.
3. **Show the fix without days of spec work.** A rough redesign that answers those questions persuades better than a proposal, but it usually costs a designer a day or two per prospect. You can sketch it by hand, or use Clikiti: the scan is free and shows any site's problems in about a minute, and paid plans draft a redesign of the home page from the prospect's own brand, from about $2.33 per pitch.

## Method

- **Sample:** 185 independent moving company websites (74 local residential movers, 36 long-distance and interstate movers, 41 moving and storage companies, 34 specialty movers: piano, senior and office).
- **When:** scanned October 2026; published 2026-10-15.
- **How:** the questions are read from the served HTML of each home page and the pages it links to. Load time, contrast and image descriptions come from Google Lighthouse (PageSpeed Insights) in a phone and a desktop render, with Chrome UX Report real-user data where it exists. A check passes only when the scan finds it; each check cites the standard it comes from.
- **Score:** Clikiti's overall website score (rubric v2): usability 30%, speed 25%, accessibility 25%, SEO 10%, trust 10%. It measures the website, not the independent moving company. The questions are reported separately and do not feed it.
- **Margin of error:** shares are exact for the sites scanned. Read as estimates for all independent moving company websites, they carry a margin of error of up to ±7.2 percentage points at 95% confidence (worst case, p = 0.5, n = 185).
- **Privacy:** results are published as aggregates or anonymously. Sites are listed by name in the appendix with no individual results; only the best-in-class example is named with its results.

## Appendix A: Methodology

185 websites, 4 types of organisation, 41 states, read on 2026-10-15. Margin of error ±7.2 points at 95% confidence.

### A1. The research question

When someone planning a move looks up an independent moving company online, usually on a phone, can they get a quote without calling, find out whether the price is fixed, what an hour or a move costs, whether the company is licensed to move them, what happens if something breaks, whether it will pack for them, whether it is free on their date, what the deposit and cancellation terms are, and whether other customers vouch for it?

Population: Independently owned US moving companies with one to three locations, their own trucks and crews and their own website, in four groups: local residential movers, long-distance and interstate movers, moving and storage companies, and specialty movers (piano, senior and office). These are the moving businesses a small or mid-size web agency can realistically win as clients.

### A2. Who was studied

| Source | What it contributes | Sites |
|---|---|---:|
| Local search, local residential movers | Independent local and residential moving companies found in local search results, city by city | 185 |
| Local search, long-distance and interstate movers | Independent long-distance and interstate moving companies found in local search results, city by city | 28 |
| Local search, moving and storage | Independent moving companies that run their own storage warehouse, found in local search results, city by city | 31 |
| Local search, specialty movers | Independent piano movers, senior move managers with crews, and office and commercial movers found in local search results, city by city | 67 |

Included:
- An independently owned business with one to three locations and its own website (not a directory, marketplace or social profile)
- Moves people's belongings with its own crews and trucks: not a broker, a lead service, a truck or container rental or a labour-only marketplace
- Based in the United States
- Home page responds and shows the business it claims to be (each home page title was checked before scanning)

Excluded:
- Van lines and their agents' national brand pages (for example Allied, North American, Atlas, Mayflower, United, Bekins, Wheaton) and franchises (for example Two Men and a Truck, College Hunks Hauling Junk and Moving, All My Sons, Gentle Giant, Mighty Movers), and any business with four or more locations
- Truck and container rental companies (for example U-Haul, PODS, U-Pack) and labour-only marketplaces
- Moving brokers and lead-generation sites: any site that sells leads, books other carriers or says it connects customers with movers
- Directories and review sites (for example Yelp, Angi, Thumbtack, moving.com, Moving Authority) and generic keyword domains that hide the business
- Parked, placeholder, under-maintenance, password-protected or unreachable sites, hijacked domains and duplicates

### A3. How the sites were read

- Pages: The home page, then for each of eleven topics (services, quote or estimate, prices or rates, packing, storage, long distance, insurance or valuation, FAQ, policies or terms, reviews, contact) the best-matching link on the home page.
- Speed and accessibility: Google PageSpeed Insights (Lighthouse) on a mid-range phone profile and on desktop, plus Chrome UX Report data from real visitors over the previous 28 days where Google publishes it for the site.
- Crawler conduct: One request at a time to each site, at least 400 ms apart, at most twelve pages per site; robots.txt is read and obeyed for every page requested; a site that refuses is left out and never retried. User agent: `Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; ClikitiResearch/1.0; +https://clikiti.com)`.

### A5. The website score

Clikiti website score, rubric 2.0.0: a weighted mean of usability 30%, speed 25%, accessibility 25%, search 10% and trust 10%. It measures the website, not the organisation; the questions above do not feed it. Report-card letters: A 90+, B 75+, C 45+, D 30+, F below 30.

### A6. Precision

±7.2 points overall at 95% confidence (worst case p = 0.5, 1.96 × √(0.25/n)); by type: local residential movers ±11.4 (n = 74); long-distance and interstate movers ±16.3 (n = 36); moving and storage companies ±15.3 (n = 41); specialty movers: piano, senior and office ±16.8 (n = 34).

### A7. Quality control

- Every home page in the cohort was fetched and its title checked against the listed business before scanning, and every home page was checked for gambling, pharmacy, adult and crypto spam
- Businesses were checked for duplicates across domains by phone number
- Detection rules were checked against the evidence they record for a sample of passes and fails for every rule, and revised before the final run
- Every published figure is recomputed from the data at build time, and a statement in the text that the data does not support stops the build
- Results are published only after automated checks for missing data, anonymity, accessibility of the report itself and the margin of error statement pass

### A8. Limitations

- A point-in-time snapshot: sites change, and a site may have improved or declined since it was read. Moving is seasonal, and the scan ran in October, after the summer peak, when some companies run off-season offers.
- Only public pages were read: the home page and pages it links to. Chat widgets, multi-step quote forms, inventory tools and customer portals were not tested.
- Answers are read from the page as a server delivers it. Content that only appears after scripts run in the browser (some whole sites, quote calculators and review widgets) is not seen, which the study counts as a gap a search engine or a slow phone would also meet.
- The study reports what a website shows. It does not judge any company's moves, crews or prices, and it does not check any USDOT or MC number, licence, insurance or valuation offer against a register or a policy.
- Automated accessibility tests find only part of what WCAG covers; keyboard and screen-reader use need a manual audit.
- The sample is a structured list of real, independent businesses, not a random draw. Margins of error are given as a guide to precision, not as a guarantee that the results hold for every business in the country.
