Clikiti scanned 165 independent home-service trade websites in October 2026 and checked each one against 13 checks: 9 questions homeowners ask before choosing, plus accessibility and speed checks tied to WCAG 2.2 and Core Web Vitals.

## Key findings

- Only 17% of the 165 independent home-service trade websites we scanned state a warranty with its terms: on parts or labour, in months or years.
- A typical site answers four of the nine questions homeowners ask before choosing (mean 4).
- The median overall score is 77 out of 100 (mean 78).
- The least-answered questions are "What if it fails again?" (17%), "What will the visit cost?" (18%) and "Can I spread the cost?" (27%).
- The best-answered are "Do you work in my area?" (80%) and "How do I reach you, and when are you open?" (64%).
- By kind of business, the highest median score is 83 (appliance repair and handyman), against 75 for plumbing and hvac, 75 for electrical and 75 for roofing and exteriors.
- 93% fail "Loads in under 2.5 s on a phone" (Core Web Vitals · LCP).
- 73% fail "Text contrast passes 4.5:1" (WCAG 1.4.3).
- 61% fail "Plumber, HVACBusiness, Electrician or RoofingContractor structured data" (Schema.org · HomeAndConstructionBusiness).

### 5 fixable problems to lead a pitch with

1. **Loads in under 2.5 s on a phone** (93% of sites, build). Fix: Resize and compress the hero image, defer scripts, aim for under 2.5 s on a phone. Pitch: "Your home page takes several seconds to appear on the phone of someone standing in a flooded basement."
2. **What if it fails again?** (83% of sites, quick fix). Fix: State the warranty on parts and on labour, in months or years, on the home page and the service pages. Pitch: ""What if it fails again?" is the second question a homeowner asks, and your site has no answer."
3. **What will the visit cost?** (82% of sites, content). Fix: Publish the service-call or diagnostic fee, and a starting price or range for the common jobs. Pitch: "Homeowners fear the bill first. Your site gives them no number at all, not even the trip charge."
4. **Can I spread the cost?** (73% of sites, content). Fix: A financing page that names the lender and the terms, linked from big-ticket service pages. Pitch: "A new furnace or roof is a five-figure decision. Your site never says it can be paid monthly."
5. **Text contrast passes 4.5:1** (73% of sites, quick fix). Fix: Raise text contrast to at least 4.5:1 in the theme. Pitch: "Parts of the site are hard to read, and it is a WCAG failure."

### How to show a prospect their problems and a redesign

1. **Lead with their problem, not your services.** Open with one question the site fails, for example loads in under 2.5 s on a phone (93% of sites in this report miss it), and show the exact page where the answer should be.
2. **Put a number on it.** Compare their site with the sector: across 165 independent plumbing, HVAC, electrical, roofing and appliance repair businesses sites, the median scores 77 out of 100 and answers 4 of the 9 questions homeowners ask. A number travels further inside the client than an opinion.
3. **Show the fix without days of spec work.** A rough redesign that answers those questions persuades better than a proposal, but it usually costs a designer a day or two per prospect. You can sketch it by hand, or use Clikiti: the scan is free and shows any site's problems in about a minute, and paid plans draft a redesign of the home page from the prospect's own brand, from about $2.33 per pitch.

## Method

- **Sample:** 165 independent home-service trade websites (44 plumbing and hvac, 42 electrical, 42 roofing and exteriors, 37 appliance repair and handyman).
- **When:** scanned October 2026; published 2026-10-15.
- **How:** the questions are read from the served HTML of each home page and the pages it links to. Load time, contrast and image descriptions come from Google Lighthouse (PageSpeed Insights) in a phone and a desktop render, with Chrome UX Report real-user data where it exists. A check passes only when the scan finds it; each check cites the standard it comes from.
- **Score:** Clikiti's overall website score (rubric v2): usability 30%, speed 25%, accessibility 25%, SEO 10%, trust 10%. It measures the website, not the independent home-service trade. The questions are reported separately and do not feed it.
- **Margin of error:** shares are exact for the sites scanned. Read as estimates for all independent home-service trade websites, they carry a margin of error of up to ±7.6 percentage points at 95% confidence (worst case, p = 0.5, n = 165).
- **Privacy:** results are published as aggregates or anonymously. Sites are listed by name in the appendix with no individual results; only the best-in-class example is named with its results.

## Appendix A: Methodology

165 websites, 4 types of organisation, 35 states, read on 2026-10-14 to 2026-10-15. Margin of error ±7.6 points at 95% confidence.

### A1. The research question

When a homeowner has a leak, a dead furnace, a storm-damaged roof, a tripped panel or a broken washer and looks up an independent contractor online, usually on a phone, can they find out how to reach it and when it is open, whether it works in their area, whether it comes in an emergency, roughly what a visit costs, whether it is licensed, whether they can book without calling, whether there is financing, whether the work is guaranteed and whether neighbours vouch for it?

Population: Independently owned US home-service contractors with one to three locations and their own website, in four groups: plumbing and HVAC, electrical, roofing and exteriors, and appliance repair and handyman services. These are the trades businesses a small or mid-size web agency can realistically win as clients.

### A2. Who was studied

| Source | What it contributes | Sites |
|---|---|---:|
| Local search, plumbing and HVAC | Independent plumbing, heating and air-conditioning contractors found in local search results, city by city | 77 |
| Local search, electrical | Independent electrical contractors and electricians found in local search results, city by city | 42 |
| Local search, roofing and exteriors | Independent roofing, siding and exterior contractors found in local search results, city by city | 42 |
| Local search, appliance repair and handyman | Independent in-home appliance repair services found in local search results, city by city; in two cities where appliance repair ran short, independent handyman and home-repair services | 37 |

Included:
- An independently owned business with one to three locations, all in one state, and its own website (not a directory, marketplace or social profile)
- Serves homeowners: residential work is a main line of business, not a side to commercial or industrial contracting
- Based in the United States
- Home page responds and shows the business it claims to be (each home page title was checked before scanning)

Excluded:
- Franchises and national brands (for example Mr. Rooter, Roto-Rooter, ARS/Rescue Rooter, One Hour Heating & Air, Benjamin Franklin Plumbing, Mister Sparky, Mr. Electric, Mr. Appliance, Sears Home Services, Handyman Connection, Mr. Handyman) and any business with four or more locations or offices in more than one state
- Contractors whose work is mostly commercial, industrial or institutional
- Retail stores whose site is a store catalogue, even when they also repair
- Directories, marketplaces and lead-generation pages (Angi, HomeAdvisor, Thumbtack, Yelp, Porch) and generic keyword domains that hide the business
- Parked, placeholder, under-maintenance, password-protected or unreachable sites, and duplicates

### A3. How the sites were read

- Pages: The home page, then for each of nine topics (services, service area, prices and specials, financing, warranty, about, reviews, booking or estimate, contact) the best-matching link on the home page.
- Speed and accessibility: Google PageSpeed Insights (Lighthouse) on a mid-range phone profile and on desktop, plus Chrome UX Report data from real visitors over the previous 28 days where Google publishes it for the site.
- Crawler conduct: One request at a time to each site, at least 400 ms apart, at most ten pages per site; robots.txt is read and obeyed for every page requested; a site that refuses is left out and never retried. User agent: `Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; ClikitiResearch/1.0; +https://clikiti.com)`.

### A5. The website score

Clikiti website score, rubric 2.0.0: a weighted mean of usability 30%, speed 25%, accessibility 25%, search 10% and trust 10%. It measures the website, not the organisation; the questions above do not feed it. Report-card letters: A 90+, B 75+, C 45+, D 30+, F below 30.

### A6. Precision

±7.6 points overall at 95% confidence (worst case p = 0.5, 1.96 × √(0.25/n)); by type: plumbing and hvac ±14.8 (n = 44); electrical ±15.1 (n = 42); roofing and exteriors ±15.1 (n = 42); appliance repair and handyman ±16.1 (n = 37).

### A7. Quality control

- Every home page in the cohort was fetched and its title checked against the listed business before scanning, and every home page was checked for gambling, pharmacy, adult and crypto spam (none found)
- Businesses were checked for duplicates across domains by phone number (none found)
- Detection rules were checked against the evidence they record for a sample of passes and fails for every rule, and revised before the final run
- Every published figure is recomputed from the data at build time, and a statement in the text that the data does not support stops the build
- Results are published only after automated checks for missing data, anonymity, accessibility of the report itself and the margin of error statement pass

### A8. Limitations

- A point-in-time snapshot: sites change, and a site may have improved or declined since it was read.
- Only public pages were read: the home page and pages it links to. Chat widgets, multi-step quote forms and customer portals were not tested.
- Answers are read from the page as a server delivers it. Content that only appears after scripts run in the browser (some whole sites, schedulers and review widgets) is not seen, which the study counts as a gap a search engine or a slow phone would also meet.
- 34 candidates blocked or did not answer automated visitors and are not in the study; sites behind such protection may differ from those studied.
- The study reports what a website shows. It does not judge any contractor's work or prices, and it does not check whether any licence is valid or current.
- Automated accessibility tests find only part of what WCAG covers; keyboard and screen-reader use need a manual audit.
- The sample is a structured list of real, independent businesses, not a random draw. Margins of error are given as a guide to precision, not as a guarantee that the results hold for every business in the country.
