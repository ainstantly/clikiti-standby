    05Method in brief

    How the sites were read

    Step 1 · all 165 sites

    Read

    The home page and up to nine pages it links to (services, service area, prices, financing, warranty, about, reviews, booking, contact), read as a server delivers them. The homeowner questions are answered here, by fixed published rules (Appendix A4).

    Step 2 · all 165 sites

    Measure

    Google Lighthouse (PageSpeed Insights) in a phone and a desktop render, with Chrome UX Report field data where it exists: load time, contrast and image descriptions. Repeatable.

    Step 3 · all 165 sites, score only

    Review

    An expert usability review of each home page against Nielsen's heuristics, used only in the usability part of the score. Every issue must name the heuristic, a severity and the element, or it is dropped.

    ScopeThis study examines how independent contractors present themselves on the web, not the quality of their work, their prices or whether a licence is valid.

    what to fix first, and how to pitch it

    For small agencies and freelancers who sell websites to independent plumbers, HVAC firms, electricians, roofers and appliance repairers. We scanned 165 of their websites: 0% score under 60 out of 100, and 85% leave at least three of the questions homeowners ask unanswered. Here is where the winnable work is, which problems to lead a pitch with, and how to show them without days of spec design.

    Plumbing and HVAC are the most winnable: the lowest median score in this report (75 out of 100), and 0% of them score under 60. Their problems are visible, specific and fixable by a small team.

    5 problems to lead a pitch with

    1. 93%
      Loads in under 2.5 s on a phone Build

      Resize and compress the hero image, defer scripts, aim for under 2.5 s on a phone.

      Your home page takes several seconds to appear on the phone of someone standing in a flooded basement.
    2. 83%
      What if it fails again? Quick fix

      State the warranty on parts and on labour, in months or years, on the home page and the service pages.

      "What if it fails again?" is the second question a homeowner asks, and your site has no answer.
    3. 82%
      What will the visit cost? Content

      Publish the service-call or diagnostic fee, and a starting price or range for the common jobs.

      Homeowners fear the bill first. Your site gives them no number at all, not even the trip charge.
    4. 73%
      Can I spread the cost? Content

      A financing page that names the lender and the terms, linked from big-ticket service pages.

      A new furnace or roof is a five-figure decision. Your site never says it can be paid monthly.
    5. 73%

      Raise text contrast to at least 4.5:1 in the theme.

      Parts of the site are hard to read, and it is a WCAG failure.

    A1. The research question

    When a homeowner has a leak, a dead furnace, a storm-damaged roof, a tripped panel or a broken washer and looks up an independent contractor online, usually on a phone, can they find out how to reach it and when it is open, whether it works in their area, whether it comes in an emergency, roughly what a visit costs, whether it is licensed, whether they can book without calling, whether there is financing, whether the work is guaranteed and whether neighbours vouch for it?

    Population. Independently owned US home-service contractors with one to three locations and their own website, in four groups: plumbing and HVAC, electrical, roofing and exteriors, and appliance repair and handyman services. These are the trades businesses a small or mid-size web agency can realistically win as clients.

    A2. Who was studied

    The sample was built from local search listings, city by city, then screened so that every site in it is one an independent agency could realistically win.

    Included

    • An independently owned business with one to three locations, all in one state, and its own website (not a directory, marketplace or social profile)
    • Serves homeowners: residential work is a main line of business, not a side to commercial or industrial contracting
    • Based in the United States
    • Home page responds and shows the business it claims to be (each home page title was checked before scanning)

    Excluded

    • Franchises and national brands (for example Mr. Rooter, Roto-Rooter, ARS/Rescue Rooter, One Hour Heating & Air, Benjamin Franklin Plumbing, Mister Sparky, Mr. Electric, Mr. Appliance, Sears Home Services, Handyman Connection, Mr. Handyman) and any business with four or more locations or offices in more than one state
    • Contractors whose work is mostly commercial, industrial or institutional
    • Retail stores whose site is a store catalogue, even when they also repair
    • Directories, marketplaces and lead-generation pages (Angi, HomeAdvisor, Thumbtack, Yelp, Porch) and generic keyword domains that hide the business
    • Parked, placeholder, under-maintenance, password-protected or unreachable sites, and duplicates

    A3. How the sites were read

    Pages. The home page, then for each of nine topics (services, service area, prices and specials, financing, warranty, about, reviews, booking or estimate, contact) the best-matching link on the home page.

    Speed and accessibility. Google PageSpeed Insights (Lighthouse) on a mid-range phone profile and on desktop, plus Chrome UX Report data from real visitors over the previous 28 days where Google publishes it for the site.

    Crawler conduct. One request at a time to each site, at least 400 ms apart, at most ten pages per site; robots.txt is read and obeyed for every page requested; a site that refuses is left out and never retried. The crawler identifies itself as Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; ClikitiResearch/1.0; +https://clikiti.com).

    A5. The website score

    Each site's overall score (0 to 100) is Clikiti's website score, rubric 2.0.0. It measures the website, not the business, and the homeowner questions above do not feed it. It is a weighted mean of five pillars:

    PillarWeightBased on
    Usability30%Checks of the home page for broken links and images, and an expert review of the page as rendered on a phone and on a desktop screen, against Jakob Nielsen's ten usability heuristics. Each issue must name the heuristic, a severity on the Nielsen Norman Group scale and the exact element or wording; issues without all three are discarded, and two independent passes must agree.
    Speed25%Google Lighthouse on a mid-range phone (60%) and on desktop (20%), and real-visitor Core Web Vitals from the Chrome UX Report (20%) where available.
    Accessibility25%Google Lighthouse accessibility audits (70%) and Clikiti's own WCAG checks of the served page (30%).
    Search10%Google Lighthouse SEO audits (60%) and Clikiti's own search checks of the served page (40%).
    Trust10%Google Lighthouse best-practice audits (40%) and security header checks (60%).

    Within a pillar, each issue costs points by severity (critical, high, medium, low) and by how many times it occurs; issues based on judgement count at 70% of measured ones, and duplicates of the same root cause count once. On the four business profiles, the number out of 100 is this overall score, and each dot is one check in its group: filled when the site passes it, hollow when it does not.

    A6. Precision

    With 165 sites, a share reported for the whole sample is precise to within ±7.6 percentage points at 95% confidence (worst case, a 50% share; 1.96 × √(0.25/n)). By type: plumbing and hvac ±14.8 (n = 44); electrical ±15.1 (n = 42); roofing and exteriors ±15.1 (n = 42); appliance repair and handyman ±16.1 (n = 37). Differences between types smaller than these margins should be read as similar. Percentages are rounded to whole numbers; medians are the middle value of the sites in a group.

    A7. Quality control

    • Every home page in the cohort was fetched and its title checked against the listed business before scanning, and every home page was checked for gambling, pharmacy, adult and crypto spam (none found)
    • Businesses were checked for duplicates across domains by phone number (none found)
    • Detection rules were checked against the evidence they record for a sample of passes and fails for every rule, and revised before the final run
    • Every published figure is recomputed from the data at build time, and a statement in the text that the data does not support stops the build
    • Results are published only after automated checks for missing data, anonymity, accessibility of the report itself and the margin of error statement pass

    A8. Limitations

    • A point-in-time snapshot: sites change, and a site may have improved or declined since it was read.
    • Only public pages were read: the home page and pages it links to. Chat widgets, multi-step quote forms and customer portals were not tested.
    • Answers are read from the page as a server delivers it. Content that only appears after scripts run in the browser (some whole sites, schedulers and review widgets) is not seen, which the study counts as a gap a search engine or a slow phone would also meet.
    • 34 candidates blocked or did not answer automated visitors and are not in the study; sites behind such protection may differ from those studied.
    • The study reports what a website shows. It does not judge any contractor's work or prices, and it does not check whether any licence is valid or current.
    • Automated accessibility tests find only part of what WCAG covers; keyboard and screen-reader use need a manual audit.
    • The sample is a structured list of real, independent businesses, not a random draw. Margins of error are given as a guide to precision, not as a guarantee that the results hold for every business in the country.

    About this study
