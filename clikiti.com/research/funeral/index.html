Summary of findings

Families ask ten things. The median home answers five.

Across 175 independent funeral home websites, the median site answers five of the ten family questions this edition tests. 39% publish or offer the General Price List; 37% show a price for cremation or a burial.1

        5 · Method in brief

        How the sites were read

        Step 1 · all 175 sites

        Read

        The home page and up to fourteen pages it links to (price list, cremation, burial, services, when a death occurs, obituaries, livestreams, pre-planning, traditions, grief support, about, reviews, contact and directions), read as a server delivers them. The family questions are answered here, by fixed published rules (Appendix A4).

        Step 2 · all 175 sites

        Measure

        Google Lighthouse (PageSpeed Insights) in a phone and a desktop render, with Chrome UX Report field data where it exists: load time, contrast and image descriptions. Repeatable.

        Step 3 · all 175 sites, score only

        Review

        An expert usability review of each home page against Nielsen's heuristics, used only in the usability part of the score. Every issue must name the heuristic, a severity and the element, or it is dropped.

        ScopeThis study examines how independent funeral homes present themselves on the web, not the care they give, their services or their prices, and it is not a judgement of compliance with the FTC Funeral Rule or any state law.

        1. 87%
          Can you honour our faith or culture? Content

          A traditions page that names the faiths and cultures the home serves, the customs it can arrange and the clergy and communities it works with.

        2. 86%
          Text contrast passes 4.5:1 Quick fix

          Raise text contrast to at least 4.5:1 in the theme, including obituary text.

        3. 78%
          Can family far away watch? Quick fix

          Say whether services can be watched online, and put each stream link on the obituary page for that service.

          Publish starting prices for direct cremation, a cremation with a service and a traditional funeral with burial, each with what it includes.

        A1. The research question

        When someone dies, or when someone plans ahead, and a family looks an independent funeral home up online, usually on a phone and often late at night, can they find the General Price List, what cremation or a burial costs, a number to call at any hour, the obituaries and service times, whether a service can be watched from far away, how to plan ahead, whether the home can honour their faith or culture, what help there is afterwards, how to get there, and whether other families vouch for the home?

        Population. Independently owned US funeral homes with one to three locations and their own website, in four groups: single-location family funeral homes, family firms with two or three locations, cremation-focused independent providers with their own local office, and funeral homes that serve a faith or cultural community. These are the funeral businesses a small or mid-size web agency can realistically win as clients.

        The sample was built from local search listings, city by city, then screened so that every site in it is one an independent agency could realistically win.

        A3. How the sites were read

        Pages. The home page, then for each of fourteen topics (price list, cremation, burial and funeral services, services, when a death occurs, obituaries, livestreams, pre-planning, religious and cultural traditions, grief support, about, reviews, contact, directions) the best-matching link on the home page.

        Speed and accessibility. Google PageSpeed Insights (Lighthouse) on a mid-range phone profile and on desktop, plus Chrome UX Report data from real visitors over the previous 28 days where Google publishes it for the site.

        Crawler conduct. One request at a time to each site, at least 400 ms apart, at most fifteen pages per site; robots.txt is read and obeyed for every page requested; a site that refuses is left out and never retried. The crawler identifies itself as Mozilla/5.0 (compatible; ClikitiResearch/1.0; +https://clikiti.com).

        A5. The website score

        Each site's overall score (0 to 100) is Clikiti's website score, rubric 2.0.0. It measures the website, not the funeral home, and the family questions above do not feed it. It is a weighted mean of five pillars:

        PillarWeightBased on
        Usability30%Checks of the home page for broken links and images, and an expert review of the page as rendered on a phone and on a desktop screen, against Jakob Nielsen's ten usability heuristics. Each issue must name the heuristic, a severity on the Nielsen Norman Group scale and the exact element or wording; issues without all three are discarded, and two independent passes must agree.
        Speed25%Google Lighthouse on a mid-range phone (60%) and on desktop (20%), and real-visitor Core Web Vitals from the Chrome UX Report (20%) where available.
        Accessibility25%Google Lighthouse accessibility audits (70%) and Clikiti's own WCAG checks of the served page (30%).
        Search10%Google Lighthouse SEO audits (60%) and Clikiti's own search checks of the served page (40%).
        Trust10%Google Lighthouse best-practice audits (40%) and security header checks (60%).

        Within a pillar, each issue costs points by severity (critical, high, medium, low) and by how many times it occurs; issues based on judgement count at 70% of measured ones, and duplicates of the same root cause count once. On the four funeral home profiles, the number out of 100 is this overall score, and each dot is one check in its group: filled when the site passes it, hollow when it does not.

        A6. Precision

        With 175 sites, a share reported for the whole sample is precise to within ±7.4 percentage points at 95% confidence (worst case, a 50% share; 1.96 × √(0.25/n)). By type: family funeral homes, one location ±12.3 (n = 64); family firms with two or three locations ±13.3 (n = 54); cremation-focused providers ±16.1 (n = 37); faith and cultural community homes ±21.9 (n = 20). Differences between types smaller than these margins should be read as similar. Percentages are rounded to whole numbers; medians are the middle value of the sites in a group.

        A7. Quality control

        • Every home page in the cohort was fetched and its title checked against the listed funeral home before scanning
        • Every home page was checked for hijacked content (gambling, pharmacy, adult and crypto spam) before scanning
        • Every home page was checked for a second website of the same funeral home (shared phone numbers and names)
        • Detection rules were checked against the evidence they record for a sample of funeral homes in every group, and revised before the final run
        • Every published figure is recomputed from the data at build time, and a statement in the text that the data does not support stops the build
        • Results are published only after automated checks for missing data, anonymity, accessibility of the report itself and the margin of error statement pass

        A8. Limitations

        • A point-in-time snapshot: sites change, and a site may have improved or declined since it was read.
        • Only public pages were read: the home page and pages it links to. Price lists inside PDF files were counted when linked, but their contents were not read.
        • Answers are read from the page as a server delivers it. Content that only appears after scripts run in the browser (some obituary and review widgets) is not seen, which the study counts as a gap a search engine or a slow phone would also meet.
        • 10 candidates blocked automated visitors or served them a bot challenge and are not in the study; sites behind such protection may differ from those studied.
        • The study reports what a website shows. It does not judge any funeral home's care, services or prices, it is not legal advice, and it does not judge compliance with the FTC Funeral Rule or any state law.
        • Automated accessibility tests find only part of what WCAG covers; keyboard and screen-reader use need a manual audit.
        • The sample is a structured list of real, independent funeral homes, not a random draw. Margins of error are given as a guide to precision, not as a guarantee that the results hold for every funeral home in the country.
        • Most candidates outside the first local searches came from a memorial supplier's partner directory and a Jewish funeral directors' member list; homes that work with such a supplier or belong to such an association may differ from other independent homes.
