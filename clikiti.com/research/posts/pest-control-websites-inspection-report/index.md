A good pest inspection ends with a form on the kitchen table. Each item is marked clear, monitor or action, and the homeowner can see at a glance what is fine, what to keep an eye on and what needs doing. We used the same form for the websites of the companies that do the inspecting.

[The State of Independent Pest Control Websites 2026](/research/pest) scanned 155 independent companies across the US: 45 general household pest companies, 36 termite companies, 39 wildlife and rodent removal companies and 35 mosquito, bed bug and specialty companies. Each site was read against the nine questions a homeowner asks before letting a technician into the house. The item that most needs action: **only 12% of the sites show a state licence number.**

::stat{ref="pest/2026-q4#licensed"}

## How were the grades set?

Each question has a written pass rule, published in the report's method. A site passes when the scan finds the answer on its home page or a page the home page links to. The grades are the report's own, from the homeowner checklist at the top of it, and they describe the industry, not any one company:

- **Clear:** half of the sites or more answer it.
- **Monitor:** between a third and half answer it.
- **Action:** fewer than a third answer it.

For an agency the grades sort the conversation. A clear item is one to confirm on a prospect's site and move past, though "clear" here only means most sites pass, so plenty of prospects still fail it. A monitor item is one most prospects fail: check it every time. An action item is where a pitch starts, because two in three prospects or more fail it.

The median site answers four of the nine.

## Clear: what most sites already get right

**The pests treated (97%).** Nearly every site says what it handles. Termite and wildlife companies pass every time, often because the answer is in the company's name. No agency needs to sell this, and a prospect who fails it has a thin site with bigger problems.

**The service area (70%).** Most sites name the towns or counties they cover. The exception is the specialty group, where 54% do. A specialty company often works a wider area than a household company, which is a reason to name it, not to leave it out.

**Booking without calling (59%).** A pass is any way to request an inspection, a quote or a visit online: a form, a button to the company's scheduling software or an embedded calendar. Household companies lead at 67%; wildlife companies sit at 51%, which is close to a coin toss.

**Reviews on the site (54%).** A pass is review markup, a review widget or quoted customer reviews. Household companies again lead (67%), wildlife companies trail (41%). Most of these companies have reviews somewhere. The gap is showing them where a homeowner is deciding.

## Monitor: the items most sites miss

**Same-day or emergency service (41%).** Wildlife companies say it most often (49%), specialty companies least (26%). A company that does come the same day and doesn't say so is leaving out its strongest line.

**Safety for children and pets (38%).** A pass needs a sentence connecting the treatment to children or pets. Most passes we read are a single reassurance line; only a couple say when the family can go back into a treated room. Specialty companies pass most often (51%), wildlife companies least (23%). So the grade is generous: a site with one line passes, and 62% don't have that line. Our [safety page playbook](/research/posts/pest-control-safety-page-spec) sets out what a full answer contains.

## Action: the three items to raise first

**The licence number (12%).** A homeowner letting a stranger treat their home has one thing they can check: the state licence. 88% of the sites give no number to check. Termite companies (8%) and specialty companies (9%) show it least. The number is printed on paperwork the company already uses, so this is the smallest fix in the report.

**The guarantee, with its terms (32%).** "Satisfaction guaranteed" doesn't pass. Free re-treatment between visits, money back, or a warranty for a stated period does. Household companies lead at 40%; wildlife companies, whose exclusion work often carries a warranty, state one on 23% of sites.

**A price (21%).** A pass is a dollar amount tied to a visit, a plan or an inspection. Coupons don't count. Household companies show one most often (36%), because a recurring plan has a recurring price. Wildlife companies almost never do (3%), since most removals start with an inspection. That inspection fee is a price, and it passes.

## What about the site underneath?

The same grading works for the four technical checks:

| Item | Sites that pass | Grade |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Images described for screen readers | 79% | Clear |
| LocalBusiness structured data | 57% | Clear |
| Text contrast passes WCAG 4.5:1 | 23% | Action |
| Loads in under 2.5 seconds on a phone | 12% | Action |

The median overall website score is 77 out of 100. Specialty companies have the lowest median of the four groups, and 97% of their sites take longer than 2.5 seconds to load on a phone. Speed is a build job; the content items above are not, which is why they make the better opening.

## Does any site come back clear on everything?

One does. [Berner Pest Solutions](https://bernerpest.com/) in Lexington, Kentucky, answers all nine questions and passes the speed and image checks. It publishes a price table by home size, shows its Kentucky company licence number, says it returns free between visits and states that its treatment is pet-friendly. It is the report's best in class, and a useful site to show a prospect what a finished answer looks like.

:::pitch{title="How do you use the report with a prospect?"}
1. **Run their site down the form.** Nine questions, one line each. Mark clear, monitor or action, the way their own technicians mark a house.
2. **Lead with one action item.** The licence number is the easiest to agree to: 88% of their competitors don't show one either, so it sets them apart in a day.
3. **Quote the fix, not the redesign.** Price, guarantee terms and a proper safety page are content the company already has on paper. Speed is the follow-on project.
:::
