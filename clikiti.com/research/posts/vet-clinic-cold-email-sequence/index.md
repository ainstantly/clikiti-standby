This is a sequence of three short emails an agency sends to the owner or practice manager of an independent vet clinic. Each one carries a single number from [The State of Independent Vet Clinic Websites 2026](/research/vet), our scan of 138 independent clinic websites in the US, and each number is about a question pet owners ask before they call.

The first email leads with one of the three least-answered questions in the study: **only 17% of the 138 sites show any price**, for an exam, a vaccine or a fee.

::stat{ref="vet/2026-q4#price"}

Clinic owners are busy and most of them are clinicians first. These emails are short on purpose, ask for nothing large, and never criticise the clinic's care. The study doesn't judge care, and neither should the email.

## Before you send anything

Open the clinic's site on your phone and run three checks, the same rules the study uses:

1. **Price.** Is there a dollar amount next to something named: an exam, a vaccine, a fee? "Affordable" and "call for pricing" don't count.
2. **New patients.** Does the site say whether the clinic is taking new patients? "Yes" and "not at this time" both count.
3. **After hours.** Does it say what to do when the clinic is closed, naming an emergency hospital or a number?

Write down what you found. Send only the emails whose gap is real for this clinic. If the site passes all three, don't send this sequence; it won't be true.

The kind of practice tells you where to look first. House-call vets are the most likely to show a price (44% of their sites do). General practices are the most likely to say whether they take new patients (31%), yet 43% of their sites have no after-hours line.

## Email 1: the price (day 1)

Subject lines to choose from:

- What does a visit cost?
- One number on your website
- A question your clients ask before they call

:::copy{label="Email 1"}
Hello,

We build websites for independent vet clinics. A study published this autumn read 138 of their sites the way a pet owner does, on a phone, against ten questions owners ask before they call.

The question answered least often was "What does a visit cost?" Only 17% of the sites show any price at all: an exam fee, a vaccine, anything with a dollar amount.

I looked at your site and couldn't find one either. You don't need to publish a price list. The exam fee on its own answers the question for most people.

The full study is free to read: clikiti.com/research/vet

Would it help if I sent over what that would look like on your site?
:::

Sign it with your own name, agency and phone number.

## Email 2: new patients (day 4)

Subject lines:

- Are you taking new patients?
- The question your front desk answers all day

:::copy{label="Email 2"}
Hello again,

One more finding from the same study. Only 17% of the 138 clinic sites say whether they are taking new patients.

Your site doesn't say either way. If you are taking new clients, one line on the home page tells a family new to the area they can book. If you are full, saying so is just as useful, and your team stops fielding calls that end in "sorry, not right now".

Happy to draft that line for you, no charge, so you can see whether it fits.
:::

The offer to draft the line for free is deliberate. It is ten minutes of your time and it shows the owner how you write.

## Email 3: tonight (day 9)

Subject lines:

- If something happens after you close
- Where do your clients go at night?

:::copy{label="Email 3"}
Hello,

Last note from me. In the study, 43% of general practice sites don't tell a client what to do when the clinic is closed.

I couldn't find that on your site. One short paragraph fixes it: the emergency hospital you refer to, its phone number and its address, on your home and contact pages.

If now isn't the time, no problem at all. The study stays free to read at clikiti.com/research/vet, and I'm glad to help whenever it suits you.
:::

Send the third email only if the after-hours gap is real. For a house-call vet, the number is 56% of mobile sites. For an emergency hospital, swap the email for the next gap you found; emergency sites answer the after-hours question on 83% of sites, so it is rarely theirs.

## What to send if they reply

Four replies are worth preparing for. Answer each one with the clinic's own results, never with a sales deck.

**"Tell me more."** Send the three checks from your notes, as a short list: which of the three questions the site answers and which it doesn't, with the page you looked at. Add one line on size: in the report, price is a content job, while the new-patient line and the after-hours paragraph are quick fixes.

:::copy{label="Reply: tell me more"}
Thanks for coming back to me. Here is what I found on your site, using the study's rules: no price for an exam or a vaccine; nothing on whether you're taking new patients; the after-hours number is there, which 43% of the clinic sites in the study don't have. The first two are a morning's work once you give me the exam fee and a yes or no on new clients. Shall I put them in a one-page draft?
:::

**"We don't post prices; clients shop around."** Point to the house-call vets: 44% of their sites show a price, against 12% of general practices. Publishing an exam fee is not a price war. It answers the first question an owner has, and the clinic still quotes everything else in the room.

**"We're full."** Then the new-patient line is the one to write, saying so. It counts as an answer and it saves the front desk time.

**"How much?"** Quote for the three items you found missing, as a fixed fee. Keep it small. A first job this size earns the conversation about the rest of the site.

:::pitch{title="The sequence on one card"}
1. **Check before you send.** Price, new patients, after hours: three rules, ten minutes, on your phone. Only send the emails whose gap is real.
2. **One number per email.** 17% show a price, 17% say whether they take new patients, 43% of general practice sites say nothing about after hours.
3. **Answer replies with their own results.** The clinic's three checks, the effort for each, and a fixed price for the small first job.
:::
