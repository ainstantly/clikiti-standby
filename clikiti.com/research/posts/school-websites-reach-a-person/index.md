Before an open day, the most practical question a parent has is whether they can talk to someone at the school. On most private school websites that takes more than one tap. The phone number sits in the footer as plain text, there's no email link, and nothing says when the office is staffed.

Most school websites look like this. In [The State of School Websites 2026](/research/education), Clikiti Research scanned 114 independent schools, small private schools and tutoring centres across the US. **Only 9% let a parent reach a person in one tap**, the lowest score of any question we tested.

::stat{ref="education/2026-q4#contact"}

## What did we actually check?

A site passes only when all three parts are present on the home page or a page it links to: a phone number a phone can dial with one tap, an email address that opens a new message, and office hours in plain text. Two out of three is a fail, because a parent who taps the number at 7 p.m. and hears nothing has learned nothing.

We tested six questions parents ask before choosing a school. Contact came last by a wide margin.

::chart{ref="education/2026-q4" highlight="contact"}

## What should you look for on a prospect's site?

Our scan records whether the check passes, not why it fails. On any school site, three things are quick to check from a phone: whether the number in the header or footer is a link you can tap, whether there's an email address or only a contact form, and whether office hours are written anywhere a parent would look.

The fix is a few lines in the site header, and on most school websites it's nobody's job.

> A parent who taps the number at 7 p.m. and hears nothing has learned nothing.

## Which schools are the easiest to win with this?

Independent schools have the lowest median website score in the study, 73 out of 100, and none of them score under 60. Their sites aren't broken. They're good-looking sites with the same few gaps, which is the easiest kind of client to convince with a single, specific fix.

| Type of site | Sites | Median score |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Independent schools | 58 | 73 |
| Small private schools | 31 | 75 |
| Tutoring and learning centres | 25 | 78 |

Other industries do better on the same question. Small law firms aren't great either, but [39% of 125 law firm websites](/research/posts/law-firm-websites-look-open) pair a tappable number with hours or a response time, four times the schools' rate. If you sell to both, that comparison works well in a pitch.

:::pitch{title="How do you pitch it?"}
1. **Show them their own phone screen.** Open the prospect's site on a phone, scroll to the contact details and take a screenshot. One image of a number they can't tap does more than a page of audit notes.
2. **Put a number on it.** Tell them 91% of the 114 schools in the study miss this too. It turns a criticism into a fixable industry problem, and the number travels inside the school when they forward your email.
3. **Quote the fix, not a redesign.** A header with a tappable phone, a tappable email and office hours. About an hour of work, and an easy first invoice that earns you the bigger conversation.
:::

## Does the best school website get it right?

Not quite. [Emerson Waldorf School](https://www.emersonwaldorf.org/) in Chapel Hill, North Carolina, has the best website in the study at 85 out of 100. It shows its tuition up front, lets families book a visit online and passes every speed and accessibility check. But its phone number isn't a tappable link, and its office hours aren't stated.

If the best school in the study misses it, your prospect almost certainly does too. That's your opening line.
