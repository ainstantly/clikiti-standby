This is a copy deck for one page: the consultation page of an independent med spa, laser clinic or injector-led practice. It is the page a client opens when she is nearly ready, and on most sites it is a form with a heading. The blocks below are written as finished text, in the order they sit on the page.

The numbers behind it come from [The State of Independent Med Spa Websites 2026](/research/medspa), our scan of 194 independent practices. The one to carry into the meeting: **66% of the sites show no treatment price at all.**

::stat{ref="medspa/2026-q4#price"}

## Why the consultation page?

Because it is where four of the least-answered questions in the study belong together. 66% of sites show no price. 58% never explain downtime or side effects. 56% name no physician as medical director or lead doctor. 29% don't name who performs treatments with a clinical credential. A client deciding whether to book a consultation is asking all four at once.

The gaps are not spread evenly. Laser and skin clinics name a credentialed provider on 41% of sites. Injector-led boutiques name a medical director on 23%. Cosmetic dermatology practices name their physicians (80% pass on the medical director) but show a price on 13% and offer a consultation by name on 22%. Read the prospect's own row in the report before you choose which block leads.

::chart{ref="medspa/2026-q4" highlight="director"}

## Block 1: the consultation itself

Open with what happens, how long it takes and what it costs. Add the length of the appointment to the first sentence. The study counts a consultation as offered when the site names one; the copy below goes further and answers the next three questions she has.

:::copy{label="On the page"}
**Start with a consultation.** Sit down with one of our injectors before you decide anything. We look at your skin, ask what you want to change and what you don't, and explain which treatments fit, what each one costs for you, and how long you are likely to look treated. Some clients book the same day. Many go home and think about it. Either is fine.
:::

If the practice charges for consultations, say so here, with the amount and whether it is credited to treatment. A fee that is stated is a far smaller obstacle than one discovered at check-in.

## Block 2: who performs your treatment

The study passes a site that names a provider with a clinical credential: MD, DO, NP, PA or RN. Name each injector and laser provider under this block, with credential and the treatments they perform. Photographs help; the credential is what passes.

:::copy{label="On the page"}
**Who treats you.** Every injectable and laser treatment here is performed by a licensed clinician: a registered nurse, nurse practitioner, physician assistant or physician. Below, each of our providers is listed with their licence, the treatments they perform and how long they have been injecting. You choose who you see when you book.
:::

Under it, one short card per provider: full name, credential after the name, treatments performed, years of practice. Aestheticians go on the same page under their own heading, so the distinction is clear without anyone having to ask.

## Block 3: the medical director

This is the gap with the widest split in the study, and the block that reassures the clients most likely to hesitate. Put the physician's full name, credential and board certification in the heading above this paragraph.

:::copy{label="On the page"}
**Medical oversight.** Our medical director is a licensed physician who sets the treatment protocols every provider here follows, reviews new clients' medical histories where a treatment calls for it, and is available to our team if a question comes up during or after your treatment.
:::

Only write what is true of the practice. If the physician sees clients for some treatments, say which. If the director oversees from off site, the paragraph above still holds; don't imply a doctor is in the room when one isn't.

## Block 4: prices

The pass rule is a dollar amount tied to a treatment, a unit, a syringe or a session. Discounts, gift cards and membership fees don't count, and neither do cancellation fees. Write the introduction once:

:::copy{label="On the page"}
**What treatments cost.** Neurotoxins are priced per unit, and the number of units depends on the areas treated and on your own muscles. Fillers are priced per syringe. Laser treatments and facials are priced per session, with a lower price per session in a series. Your provider confirms the total at your consultation before anything is done.
:::

Then a plain list in the practice's own numbers: each neurotoxin brand with its price per unit and member price, filler per syringe, each laser treatment per session and per series. Starting prices ("from") pass as well as exact ones. If the injectors quote a typical range of units for common areas, add it under the neurotoxin prices: the unit price and the range together let a client estimate her visit before she books.

The price is the block owners resist. The objection is usually that every face is different. The paragraph above answers it on the page: the unit price is fixed, the number of units varies, and the provider confirms the total first.

## Block 5: aftercare and downtime

58% of sites never say how long a client will look treated. One paragraph per treatment family answers it, with a link to the full aftercare sheet.

:::copy{label="On the page"}
**After your treatment.** After neurotoxin, most clients go straight back to their day; small marks at the injection points usually fade within hours, and results show over the following two weeks. After filler, expect some swelling and possible bruising for a few days, so plan around events. After laser resurfacing, your skin may be red and peel for several days. We send written aftercare instructions home with you, and you can call us with any question.
:::

Have the medical director read this block before it goes live. It is clinical guidance in plain words, and it has to match what the practice tells clients in the room.

## Block 6: the button

One booking button, repeated at the top and the bottom of the page, that opens the practice's scheduler on the consultation appointment type. 87% of the sites already have online booking; the job here is pointing it at the right appointment, not adding it.

## How long does it take?

The report rates price, provider names and downtime as content work and the medical director as a quick fix. Most of the time goes on collecting facts from the owner. Send one sheet asking for: the consultation length and fee, each provider's name and credential, the medical director's name and board certification, the price list the front desk uses, and the aftercare sheets. Once it comes back, the page is a day of writing and layout on the existing site.

:::pitch{title="How do you pitch it?"}
1. **Open with the price.** "The first thing a client wants to know is what it costs, and your site makes her book a consultation to find out." 66% of 194 practices show no price at all.
2. **Name the doctor.** 56% of the sites name no medical director. If the prospect has one, the block costs an hour and answers the hesitant client's first worry.
3. **Sell one page, not a redesign.** The consultation page answers price, oversight, who treats and downtime together, from facts the front desk already gives on the phone.
:::
