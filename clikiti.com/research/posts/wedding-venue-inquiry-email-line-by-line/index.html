The inquiry email couples write because 91% of venue sites don't show open dates

We read 191 independent wedding venue websites the way a couple does. Each question the site leaves unanswered becomes a line in the couple's first email. Here is that email, line by line, with the share of sites that make the couple write it.

The short version
  • Only 9% of 191 independent wedding venue websites show which dates are still open (Clikiti Research, October 2026).
  • A typical site answers five of the nine questions couples ask before they request a tour. 51% show no starting price, 64% state no rain plan and 54% offer no way to book or request a tour online.
  • For an agency, every unanswered question is a line the couple has to email, and a reply the venue owner has to write. The fixes are mostly a page or a paragraph each.

A couple choosing a wedding venue usually starts with a short list and a phone. When a venue's website answers their questions, the couple books a tour. When it doesn't, they write an email. That email is remarkably consistent, because the questions are.

We read the websites of 191 independent wedding venues for The State of Independent Wedding Venue Websites 2026: barns and farms, estates and historic houses, vineyards and wineries, and city lofts and restaurants with an event space. For each one we asked nine questions a couple asks before they request a tour, and checked whether the home page or a page it links to answers them. The question answered least: only 9% of the sites show which dates are still open.

9%

of the 191 websites we scanned answer “Is our date still free?” (open dates listed or an availability calendar).

Source: Clikiti Research, The State of Independent Wedding Venue Websites 2026 · n = 191 · scanned Oct 2026 · margin of error up to ±7.1 pts

So we wrote the email the other 91% receive. Each line below is a question, followed by how many of the 191 sites leave a couple to ask it.

Line one: "Is our date still free?"#

Asked by couples at 91% of the venues we studied. Only 9% list open dates or show an availability calendar a couple can read. Barns and farms do best at 18%. Among the 39 lofts and restaurants with an event space, not one site shows open dates.

A "check availability" button that opens an inquiry form did not count, and neither did a date picker inside a form. Both ask the couple for their date; neither tells them anything. For most venues, a list of the open Saturdays for the next two seasons would do, updated when a date books.

Line two: "What will it cost us?"#

Asked at 51% of venues. 49% show a starting price or a price range for the venue fee or a package. Vineyards and wineries show a price least often, on 40% of their sites; barns and farms most often, on 53%.

The study counted a fee tied to the venue: a rental by season or day of the week, a package, a price per guest or a "starting at" figure. Tasting fees, menu prices and add-on charges such as cleaning or an extra hour did not count. A couple comparing three venues on a Sunday evening can rule a venue in on a starting price. Without one, they write.

Line three: "What happens if it rains?"#

Asked at 64% of venues. 36% state a rain or weather plan. The barns that host outdoor ceremonies say it most, on 48% of their sites. Lofts and restaurants say it least, on 13%, though some of those have a roof deck or a courtyard couples will ask about.

A plan counted when it named what happens: where the ceremony moves, a covered or tented backup, "rain or shine". "Weather permitting" did not count, nor did a FAQ question whose answer wasn't on the page.

Line four: "Can we come and see it?"#

Asked at 54% of venues. 46% let a couple book or request a tour online: a scheduler, a form on the page a "book a tour" link opens, or a form with a tour field. A phone number alone did not count. Couples often plan in the evening, after the office closes.

Line five: "Have other couples been happy here?"#

Asked at 59% of venues. 41% show reviews in a widget or as structured data, or quote at least two couples in their own words. Most venues have glowing reviews somewhere else. The site is where the couple is already looking.

The lines most venues already answer#

Not every line makes it into the email. 95% of sites show a gallery of real weddings, 76% state how many guests the venue holds, 70% say whether a couple can bring their own caterer, and 59% list what the venue fee includes. Those are the questions venues are used to answering in their brochures, and most sites carry them over.

Put together, the median site answers five of the nine questions. Only 2 of the 191 answer all nine.

Every unanswered question is a line the couple has to email, and a reply the venue owner has to write.

Which venues are the best prospects?#

Look at what the venue sells first. A barn or farm that hosts outdoor ceremonies needs the rain line, and is the kind of venue most likely to already keep an availability calendar somewhere. A vineyard or winery is the most likely to hide its wedding price behind its wine and tasting pages. A loft or restaurant will usually have the catering answer (82% state a vendor policy) and almost never the date answer.

The technical side gives you a second opening. 72% of the sites fail the phone load-time check, and among barns and farms only 13% pass it. 76% have text below the WCAG contrast minimum, which is common on sites that set light text over photographs.

How do you pitch it?

  1. Write the email for them. Before the first call, read the venue's site with the nine questions and write the email a couple would have to send. Show it to the owner. They have received it many times.
  2. Start with the date. Only 9% of venues show open dates. A page of open Saturdays is the change couples will notice first, and it filters the inquiries the owner gets.
  3. Price the rest by the paragraph. The rain plan, the tour button and the quoted couples are a paragraph, a button and a few sentences each. Say so, and scope them that way.
Open your email with"Is our date free?" is the first question in every inquiry. Answer it on the site and the inquiries you get are for dates you have.
