The estimate form is where a homeowner stops reading and starts asking. Most landscaping companies already have one. The trouble is what it asks, and what it leaves the homeowner to guess, the price above all. This spec rebuilds the form field by field, with the wording for each label and the pitch for the owner.

It is built on [The State of Independent Landscaping Websites 2026](/research/landscaping), our scan of 189 independent landscaping, lawn care, hardscape and pool company websites in the US. **77% of them take a quote request online.** Only 15% show any price, so on most of these sites the form is the only way to find out what a job might cost.

::stat{ref="landscaping/2026-q4#quote"}

## What did the scan count as a quote request?

A site passes when a page we read has a form with a message, project or service field, when a "free estimate" or "request a quote" link leads to such a form, or when it uses an estimate or booking service. A phone number alone does not count, and neither does a link to a page with no form on it.

By kind of business, design and build firms are the least likely to take the request online:

| Kind of business | Sites | Takes a quote request online |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Lawn care and maintenance | 49 | 84% |
| Hardscape and outdoor living | 48 | 81% |
| Pools and irrigation | 44 | 75% |
| Design and build | 48 | 67% |

## The form, field by field

Eight fields, in this order. Only the first four are required.

**1. Name.** One field, not two.

**2. How should we reach you?** A phone or email field and a choice of which the homeowner prefers. Make one of them required, not both.

:::copy{label="On the form"}
**How should we reach you?** Phone or email, whichever you'd rather. We'll only use it about this request.
:::

**3. Address or ZIP code.** The company needs it to price the job and plan the visit. Put the service area right above it: 74% of the sites name the towns or ZIP codes they cover, and the rest leave a homeowner to find out after sending.

:::copy{label="On the form"}
**Where is the property?** A street address is best; a ZIP code is enough to start. We work in the towns listed above this form.
:::

**4. What do you need?** Checkboxes, one per service, in the homeowner's words: lawn mowing, spring cleanup, planting, a patio or walkway, a retaining wall, irrigation, pool opening. Use the list on the services page so the two match. "Something else" goes last.

**5. Tell us about the yard.** A free-text box with a prompt, not an empty rectangle.

:::copy{label="On the form"}
**Tell us about the yard.** Roughly how big, what's there now, and what you'd like it to be. A sentence or two is plenty.
:::

**6. Photos.** Optional, and say so. A photo of the front yard or the slope saves the company a trip for small jobs.

**7. When would you like it done?** Three choices: this season, next season, no fixed date. It tells the company how to sort the requests during booking season without a phone call.

**8. Budget.** The field most forms get wrong; it has its own section below.

## Budget pickers that don't hide prices

Many quote forms carry a budget dropdown: under a certain amount, a middle range, a top range, not sure. Our scan reads those amounts and does not count them as prices, and for a reason. They are the homeowner's guess, asked for before the company has said anything about cost, and the dropdown gives them nothing to base the guess on.

The fix is to put the company's own prices on the page first, as text, and let the picker follow them:

- Above the form, a short list of typical ranges for the company's common jobs: weekly mowing per visit, a cleanup, a patio per square foot, a pool opening. Use the figures the owner quotes most often, from jobs done this year.
- The picker's options match those ranges, so a homeowner can find their job in the list and pick the band that fits.
- "Not sure yet" stays as an option, and the field is never required.

:::copy{label="Above the form"}
**What things usually cost.** These are the ranges most of our jobs fell in this year. Your price depends on the site, and we'll confirm it in writing after we've seen the yard.
:::

:::copy{label="On the form"}
**Which range is closest to what you have in mind?** It helps us suggest the right design. You can change your mind after the visit.
:::

The ranges above the form are what count as a price, for a homeowner and for our scan.

## What to skip

- **Lot size in square feet.** Few homeowners know it, and the company measures on the visit anyway.
- **"How did you hear about us?" as a required field.** Ask on the call, or make it optional.
- **An account or a password.** Nobody signs up to ask for a mowing quote.
- **A required budget.** See above. A required guess loses the homeowners who don't have one.

## Next to the send button

Two lines belong beside the button. Only 29% of the sites say the business is licensed and insured or name a licence or certification, so put it here, with the number in the footer. And say what happens after sending; use the reply time the company actually keeps.

:::copy{label="Beside the button"}
**Licensed and insured.** Our licence number is at the bottom of every page.

**What happens next:** we read every request ourselves and reply by phone or email, whichever you picked, to set up a visit.
:::

## How long does it take?

The report rates the online quote request as a build job and the price as a content job. The form is a day or two on most sites. The ranges take longer, because the owner has to agree on them; ask for the five jobs they quote most often and what those jobs cost this year.

:::pitch{title="How do you pitch it?"}
1. **Show them the guess they ask for.** Open their form on your phone, point at the budget dropdown, and ask what a homeowner should pick if they have never priced the job. Then show that only 15% of the companies in the study publish any price.
2. **Offer the ranges as the fix, not a price list.** Typical ranges from this year's jobs commit the owner to nothing, and they turn the picker into a help.
3. **Ship the form first, the prices second.** The form is quick and works the week it goes live. The ranges follow when the owner has an hour.
:::
