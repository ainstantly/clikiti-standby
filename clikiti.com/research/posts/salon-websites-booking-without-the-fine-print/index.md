Online booking is the question salon websites answer best. The booking app does the heavy lifting, and the button sits in the header. So when an agency looks at a salon site and sees "Book now", it is tempting to tick the box and move on to the design.

That is the wrong place to stop. [The State of Independent Salon and Barbershop Websites 2026](/research/salons) read 193 independent hair salons, barbershops, colour and specialty salons and salons with spa services in the US, on a phone, the way a new client does. **72% let a client book a chosen stylist or barber online.** Then we looked at what each site says around that button, because a new client presses it only after a few other questions are settled.

::stat{ref="salons/2026-q4#book"}

What follows is one pair per section: the booking button on one side, one of those questions on the other.

## Can I book my stylist online?

Yes, on most sites. 72% link to a salon booking system where the client chooses the person, embed one, or offer a "book with" link for a named stylist. The share hardly moves by kind of salon: 74% of hair salons, 75% of barbershops, 68% of colour and specialty salons and 69% of salons with spa services.

This is the strongest answer in the study, and it sets the frame for everything else. The client can commit in two taps. Whether they do depends on what they could find out first.

## What does a cut or colour cost?

49% of sites show a dollar price next to a named service: a cut, a colour, a fade, a blowout, a facial. "From $65" counts. Salons with spa services show prices most often, on 69% of sites. Barbershops, where a haircut is the simplest thing in the trade to price, show one on 37%.

Many booking apps do show a price, inside the app, after the client has picked a service and a person. A price menu on the salon's own page answers the question before the client starts that flow, and it is the page a search engine can read.

## What if I need to cancel?

This is the widest gap beside the button. **Only 42% of sites state a cancellation, late or no-show policy**: a notice window, a fee, a deposit or a card held to secure the time.

::chart{ref="salons/2026-q4" highlight="policy"}

The split by kind of salon is stark. 55% of hair salons and 55% of colour and specialty salons state a policy, and 48% of salons with spa services do. Barbershops state one on 12% of sites, while 75% of them take bookings online. A barbershop that books online and says nothing about no-shows is inviting the empty chair it complains about.

A policy also protects the client. A long colour appointment is a real commitment on both sides, and a client who knows the notice window, and that a deposit holds the slot, books with less doubt.

## Can I just walk in?

20% of sites say whether the salon takes walk-ins: walk-ins welcome, walk-ins when a chair is free, or by appointment only. Barbershops and colour and specialty salons say so most often, on 24% and 26% of sites; hair salons on 13%.

The booking button implies "appointments", but it does not say "only". A client standing outside a barbershop, phone in hand, wants one line: come in, or book first.

## Whose work would I be getting?

26% of sites show the salon's own work in a gallery or portfolio page, or an embedded Instagram feed. A link out to Instagram alone does not count, because it sends the client away from the button. Colour and specialty salons show work on 36% of sites, barbershops on 10%.

A client who books a named stylist is choosing a pair of hands. The button lists the names; most sites never show what those hands have done.

## Can I get in with a wheelchair?

3% of sites say whether the salon is physically accessible: a step-free entrance, a ground floor, an elevator, accessible parking. Among hair salons the share is 0%. This is the least-answered question in the study, and the cheapest to fix: one sentence on the contact page, written by someone who has walked through the door.

A client who uses a wheelchair cannot book with confidence, however good the booking flow is, if they cannot tell whether they will get past the entrance.

## What does this mean for an agency?

The expensive part of a salon website, the booking integration, already exists on most of them. What is missing is copy, and copy is a small, fixed job an owner can say yes to quickly:

- a short price menu with starting prices, on the salon's own page;
- the cancellation policy, linked right under the booking button;
- one line on walk-ins beside the opening hours;
- a gallery grouped by stylist;
- one sentence on access.

Then the technical layer. 84% of the sites take longer than 2.5 seconds to show their main content on a phone, and 84% have no HairSalon, BarberShop or BeautySalon structured data. Both slow down the client who is ready to book.
