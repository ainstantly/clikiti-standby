People rarely look for a lawyer on a calm afternoon. They look after an arrest, an accident or a letter they didn't expect, often late, often on a phone. The first thing they need to know is whether anyone will pick up.

In [The State of Law Firm Websites 2026](/research/law), Clikiti Research scanned 125 solo and small law firms across personal injury, family and estate, criminal defence and immigration, and small business and employment law. **Only 39% pair a tappable phone number with office hours, 24/7 availability or a response time.**

::stat{ref="law/2026-q4#contact"}

## What counts as "open"?

A site passes when the phone number can be tapped to call and something beside it tells the visitor what to expect: office hours, "available 24/7", or a stated response time such as "we reply within one business hour". A number on its own isn't enough, because the caller still doesn't know whether to wait or try the next firm.

## What else do clients check first?

Whether the lawyer can practise in their state. Only 43% of the firms say where their lawyers are admitted or licensed. Reviews and case results are missing from more than half of the sites, too.

::chart{ref="law/2026-q4" highlight="contact"}

## How does this compare with other industries?

Law firms do better than schools on the same kind of question: only [9% of private school websites](/research/posts/school-websites-reach-a-person) let a parent reach a person in one tap. But 61% of firms still miss it, and for a firm the cost of a missed call is a client who signs with someone else that night.

::stat{ref="law/2026-q4#admit"}

:::pitch{title="How do you pitch it?"}
1. **Call them after hours.** Then open their site on your phone and show what a new client sees at the same moment.
2. **Put a number on it.** 61% of the 125 firms in the study miss this. It's common, which makes it easy for a partner to accept.
3. **Quote two quick fixes together.** A header with a tappable number and the hours or response time beside it, and an admission line on each lawyer's page. Both are content changes, not a redesign.
:::

## Does any firm get it all right?

One does. [Worgul, Sarna & Ness](https://www.pittsburghcriminalattorney.com/), a criminal defence and immigration firm in Pittsburgh, answers all eight client questions in the study, loads fast on phones and describes its images. Like most firms, it still fails the text contrast check, which shows how far even the best site has to go.
